# Ch 10: Sexual Dysfunctions, Paraphilic Disorders, and Gender Dysphoria

This chapter covers three conditions that DSM-5 treats as **separate diagnostic classes**: **sexual dysfunctions** (difficulty functioning during sex), **paraphilic disorders** (the new term for "sexual deviation," where arousal is focused on inappropriate objects/persons), and **gender dysphoria** (distress at the incongruence between natal sex and experienced gender; explicitly **not** a sexual disorder). It opens with a long "what is normal sexuality?" survey section (gender, cultural, and developmental data including sexual orientation), then works disorder-by-disorder through clinical description, prevalence, integrative causes, and psychosocial + medical treatment. It is a high-yield, disorder-dense and criteria-dense exam chapter.

> **DSM-5-TR note (chapter-wide):** This text is written to DSM-5 (2013). DSM-5-TR (2022) retained all three classes and kept the sexual-dysfunction and paraphilic-disorder criteria essentially unchanged. The most significant DSM-5-TR change relevant here is in the **gender-dysphoria** section: DSM-5-TR revised terminology to be more affirming (e.g., "experienced gender," **"desired gender"** rather than "the other gender"; **"individual assigned [male/female] at birth"** [AMAB/AFAB] instead of "natal male/female"; "gender-affirming medical procedure" rather than "sex reassignment surgery"). DSM-5-TR also updated ICD-10-CM codes. Criteria sets below are DSM-5 as printed in the book; flagged where DSM-5-TR or ICD-11 differs.

## What Is Normal Sexuality?

### Defining Normal vs Disordered
- **Magazine/online surveys are unreliable** (distorted half-truths, no scientific method). Real prevalence requires **random-sample surveys** (e.g., Mosher, Chandra & Jones 2005 — **12,571** U.S. men/women ages 15–44, CDC National Survey of Family Growth; interview-based, more reliable than questionnaires).
- Current views are **tolerant** of unusual sexual expression **unless** it involves (a) substantial impairment in functioning, or (b) **nonconsenting individuals** (e.g., children).
- Two kinds of behavior meet the disorder definition:
  - **Sexual dysfunction** — difficulty functioning adequately during sex (not aroused, no orgasm).
  - **Paraphilic disorder** — arousal occurs primarily with **inappropriate objects/individuals**. *Philia* = strong attraction/liking; *para* = abnormal. Arousal is narrowly focused, often **precluding consenting adult partners**.
- **Gender dysphoria** = incongruence + distress with assigned-at-birth gender. NOT sexual; a disturbance in one's **sense of being male/female**. Separated from sexual disorders altogether in DSM-5.

### Survey Prevalence Data (key exam numbers)
- Virtually all adults sexually experienced; **vaginal intercourse nearly universal**. By age 15, ~25% have had intercourse.
- Mosher: **90% of men, 88% of women** engaged in oral sex; only **40% / 35%** ever had anal sex (high AIDS-transmission risk).
- **23.3%** of men had had ≥20 partners (Billy 1993); but **>70%** had only 1 partner in the previous year; <10% had ≥4.
- Only **6.5%** of adult men ever engaged in any homosexual behavior; **92%** attracted only to females. ~9% of women / 10% of men report some homosexual attraction/behavior. ~4% chose **"something else"** (neither hetero/homo/bisexual) → categories may not capture true range.
- British (Johnson 1992) + French (Spira 1992) surveys (>20,000 each) **closely matched** U.S. data → results approximate the Western norm.
- **Old age:** sex continues past 80 for some. Lindau (2007): age 75–85, **38.5% of men, 16.7% of women** sexually active (men's earlier mortality leaves women without partners). Decreases tied to mobility, disease, medication; vasocongestive responses slow with age.

### Gender Differences (Petersen & Hyde 2010 meta-analysis)
- **Masturbation = the largest gender difference**: 98% of men vs 64% of women ever masturbated (Pinkerton 2003); frequency ~2.5× greater for men. Likely **anatomical** (erectile response makes it more convenient); difference also seen in primates/animals. Masturbation in adolescence does NOT predict later sexual functioning.
- Men more permissive re: **casual sex, "hooking up," pornography**. "Friends with benefits" linked to alcohol (esp. for women). More partners → more worry/vulnerability **for women**, opposite for men.
- **No** gender differences now in attitudes toward homosexuality, importance of sexual satisfaction, or attitudes toward masturbation. Small differences remain in premarital/extramarital approval (men more approving), number of partners, age at first intercourse.
- Premarital-sex approval among young women rose **12% (1943) → 73% (1999)**; men 40% → 79%.
- **Arousal pattern difference (Chivers 2004):** men are **narrow/specific** (hetero men aroused only by female stimuli); women (hetero or lesbian) show **broader, general** arousal to both male and female stimuli.
- **Sexual self-schemas** (Barbara Andersen): women's = passion/romance/openness, but many also hold an embarrassed/conservative/self-conscious schema; men's = powerful/independent/aggressive + passionate, without negative self-conscious core beliefs.
- Peplau's 4 themes: (1) men show more desire/arousal; (2) women emphasize committed relationships; (3) men's self-concept tied to power/aggression; (4) women's beliefs more **"plastic"** (shaped by cultural/situational factors; women more likely to change orientation over time).

### Cultural Differences
- Normal varies cross-culturally. **Sambia (Papua New Guinea):** boys from ~age 7 are exclusively homosexual (semen recipients via oral sex, believed essential for growth), switch to providers in adolescence, then become **exclusively heterosexual** adults "with no exceptions."
- **Munda (NE India):** mixed-sex children, all-heterosexual petting/mutual masturbation.
- Sweden vs U.S.: similar age at first coitus (~17), but **73.7% of Swedish vs 56.7% of American** women used contraception at first intercourse. ~half of >100 societies accept/encourage premarital sex.
- Within U.S.: Chinese/Japanese women less likely than Caucasian to rate sex as very important; African-American women more likely; Hispanic women less likely to cite "pleasure."

### Development of Sexual Orientation
- **Runs in families.** Twin studies: homosexual orientation shared in **~50% of identical twins** vs **16%–22% of fraternal twins**.
- Genetic contribution: **34%–39% in men, 18%–19% in women** (Långström 2010); remainder = environment (incl. **differential prenatal hormone/androgen exposure in utero**).
- Supporting findings (mixed/unreplicated): higher rate of **non-right-handedness** (+39%); gay/bisexual men shorter & lighter; **2D:4D finger-ratio** differences (influenced by ethnicity); a 1993 X-chromosome gene (Hamer) **not replicated**.
- **Fraternal birth order hypothesis:** each additional **older brother** raises odds of being gay by ~one-third (Blanchard); replicated; mechanism unknown.
- Conclusion: **polygenic** vulnerability × environment; **~50% of identical twins** with same genes + same home did **not** share orientation. Sexual orientation may be **malleable**, especially in women (Lisa Diamond longitudinal studies: >two-thirds of women changed identity label over 10 years, usually **broadening** their range).

> **ICD-11 note:** Homosexuality has not been a diagnosis since DSM-III-R / ICD-10; **ICD-11 (2022) eliminated the residual "ego-dystonic sexual orientation"** category entirely. Sexual orientation is explicitly **not** a disorder in any modern nosology.

## An Overview of Sexual Dysfunctions

- The **sexual response cycle** (Kaplan; Masters & Johnson — Figure 10.2): **Desire → Arousal → Plateau → Orgasm → Resolution**. Three phases (desire, arousal, orgasm) each map to specific dysfunctions; **pain** adds a fourth dysfunction in women.
- Both sexes can have parallel versions of most disorders. **Two are sex-specific:** premature (early) ejaculation (males only); genito-pelvic pain/penetration disorder (females only).
- **Specifiers (apply across dysfunctions):**
  - **Lifelong** (present entire sexual life) vs **Acquired** (after a period of normal function).
  - **Generalized** (every attempt) vs **Situational** (some partners/times only).
- **Dysfunction ≠ dissatisfaction:** Frank (1978) — 40% of men, 63% of women in happy couples reported occasional dysfunction without reduced satisfaction. Bancroft (2003): 44.3% of women met criteria for a disorder but only **24.4%** were distressed. **Best predictor of sexual distress = general emotional well-being / relationship quality**, not lack of lubrication/orgasm.
- **DSM-5 requirement:** symptoms must cause **clinically significant distress** in the individual (controversial — should a problem be diagnosed if the person isn't distressed?).

### Categories (Table 10.3)
| Phase | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|
| Desire | **Male hypoactive sexual desire disorder** | **Female sexual interest/arousal disorder** |
| Arousal | **Erectile disorder** | Female sexual interest/arousal disorder (combined) |
| Orgasm | **Delayed ejaculation**; **premature (early) ejaculation** | **Female orgasmic disorder** |
| Pain | — | **Genito-pelvic pain/penetration disorder** (incl. vaginismus) |

### Sexual Desire Disorders

**Male Hypoactive Sexual Desire Disorder (HSDD) & Female Sexual Interest/Arousal Disorder**
- Little/no interest in any sexual activity. Hard to assess — requires clinical judgment (gauge by frequency, fantasies, or unwanted sex). A person may have **frequent sex but no desire**.
- In women, low desire almost always co-occurs with **diminished arousal**, so DSM-5 **combined** interest + arousal into one female disorder.
- **Cases:** *Judy & Ira* (Ira loved wife but no arousal thinking of her, unlike his promiscuous premarital lovers); *Mr. & Mrs. C* (Mrs. C enjoyed sex once involved but avoided it; childhood sexual abuse by cousin age 6–12 triggered by husband's romantic advances).
- **Prevalence:** >50% of patients at sexuality clinics complain of low desire; most common female presenting complaint. U.S. survey: **22% of women, 5% of men**; international survey up to **43% of women**. For **men prevalence rises with age**; for **women it decreases with age**. Patients rarely fantasize, seldom masturbate (35% of women, 52% of men never), intercourse ≤ once/month.

> **DSM-5-TR Criteria — Male Hypoactive Sexual Desire Disorder (Table 10.1):**
> **A.** Persistently/recurrently deficient (or absent) sexual/erotic thoughts/fantasies AND desire for sexual activity (clinician judges deficiency, accounting for age & socio-cultural context).
> **B.** Persisted ≥ ~**6 months**. **C.** Causes clinically significant distress. **D.** Not better explained by another mental disorder, severe relationship distress/stressors, substance/medication, or medical condition.
> Specify: **Lifelong/Acquired**; **Generalized/Situational**.

> **DSM-5-TR Criteria — Female Sexual Interest/Arousal Disorder (Table 10.2):**
> **A.** Lack of / significantly reduced interest/arousal, ≥3 of: (1) reduced interest in activity; (2) reduced erotic thoughts/fantasies; (3) no/reduced initiation + unreceptive to partner; (4) reduced excitement/pleasure in ~75–100% of encounters; (5) reduced response to internal/external erotic cues; (6) reduced genital/nongenital sensations in ~75–100% of encounters.
> **B.** ≥ **6 months**. **C.** Distress. **D.** Exclusions as above. Specify Lifelong/Acquired, Generalized/Situational.

### Sexual Arousal Disorders

**Erectile Disorder** (male-specific; the desire is intact — problem is **physical arousal**)
- Old derogatory terms: **"impotence"** (men) and **"frigidity"** (women) — imprecise, don't localize the phase. Men typically feel more impaired (intercourse becomes impossible); women can compensate with **commercial lubricant**.
- Rarely total inability; more typical (case *Bill*, 58) = full erection during masturbation, partial during intercourse, insufficient rigidity to penetrate. (Bill's fix: stop pre-sex masturbating, use lubricant, delay morning routine until after sex.)
- **Prevalence rises sharply with age:** U.S. survey 5% (ages 18–59 stringent criteria) **underestimates**; **60% of men ≥60** (Rosen 2005); ~40% in 40s, **70% in 70s** (Feldman 1994). Incidence: 46 new cases/yr per 1,000 men in their 60s. **Most common male presenting complaint** (~50% of referrals).
- **Female arousal disorder prevalence:** ~14% (Laumann); hard to estimate (overlap of desire/arousal/orgasm; many women don't consider it a problem).

### Orgasm Disorders
- **Female orgasmic disorder** — inability to reach orgasm despite adequate desire/arousal; **most common complaint** among women seeking sex therapy. ~25% of women report significant difficulty. Crucially only **~20% of women reliably orgasm during intercourse** → diagnosis requires **"never or almost never"** + distress. Unmarried women 1.5× more likely. *Case: Greta & Will* (Greta never orgasmed, strict Catholic upbringing, mother punished genital touching).
- **Delayed ejaculation (male)** — orgasm only with great difficulty/not at all. ~8% of men; men seldom seek treatment (often accommodated). Distinguish from **retrograde ejaculation** (fluid travels backward into bladder — caused by drugs/medical condition, NOT delayed ejaculation).
- **Premature (early) ejaculation** — the **most common male sexual dysfunction** (**21%** of men, U.S. survey). DSM-5 defines as **~1 minute after vaginal penetration**. Patrick (2005): complainers ejaculate at **1.8 min** vs **7.3 min** in non-complainers. **Perceived lack of control** may be the key psychological determinant. Occurs mainly in **inexperienced, less-educated men**. *Case: Gary*, ejaculates within seconds 70–80% of the time.

> **DSM-5-TR Criteria — Female Orgasmic Disorder (Table 10.3):**
> **A.** On ~75–100% of occasions, either: (1) marked delay/infrequency/absence of orgasm; OR (2) markedly reduced orgasmic intensity.
> **B.** ≥ **6 months**. **C.** Distress. **D.** Exclusions. Specify Lifelong/Acquired, Generalized/Situational; specify **"Never experienced an orgasm under any situation."**

> **DSM-5-TR Criteria — Premature (Early) Ejaculation (Table 10.4):**
> **A.** Persistent/recurrent ejaculation within ~**1 minute** of vaginal penetration and before the person wishes it (duration criteria not established for nonvaginal activity).
> **B.** Present ≥ **6 months** on ~75–100% of occasions. **C.** Distress. **D.** Exclusions.

### Sexual Pain Disorder (female-specific)

**Genito-Pelvic Pain/Penetration Disorder**
- Difficulty with penetration or significant pain during intercourse; may include severe anxiety/panic in anticipation.
- **Vaginismus** = the usual presentation: **involuntary spasms** of the outer-third pelvic muscles when penetration attempted (also during gynecological exam / tampon insertion). Reported as "ripping, burning, tearing." *Case: Jill*, 23, unconsummated 1-yr marriage, vaginal spasms prevented all penetration, viewed intercourse as "disgusting."
- DSM-5 **combined** vaginismus + dyspareunia (pain during intercourse) into this one disorder (they overlap heavily).
- **Prevalence:** vaginismus ~6% of women; 25% of women with any sexual dysfunction have it. U.S. survey: ~7% have some pain disorder (higher in younger, less-educated women). DSM-5 cites ~**15%** in North America reporting recurring pain.

> **DSM-5-TR Criteria — Genito-Pelvic Pain/Penetration Disorder (Table 10.5):**
> **A.** Persistent/recurrent difficulty with ≥1 of: (1) vaginal penetration; (2) marked vulvovaginal/pelvic pain during penetration; (3) marked fear/anxiety about such pain; (4) marked tensing/tightening of pelvic-floor muscles during attempted penetration.
> **B.** ≥ **6 months**. **C.** Distress. **D.** Exclusions. Specify Lifelong/Acquired.

> **DSM-5-TR note (sexual dysfunctions):** DSM-5-TR keeps all of the above criteria unchanged. Note the two DSM-5 structural changes the chapter highlights are retained in DSM-5-TR: (a) **merging** female desire + arousal into Female Sexual Interest/Arousal Disorder, and (b) **merging** vaginismus + dyspareunia into Genito-Pelvic Pain/Penetration Disorder.

> **ICD-11 note:** ICD-11 classifies sexual dysfunctions in an integrated **"Conditions Related to Sexual Health"** chapter (jointly with mental-disorders coding) and is **less binary**: it does NOT merge desire+arousal in women the way DSM-5 does (it keeps **Hypoactive Sexual Desire Dysfunction** applicable across sexes, plus separate **Female Sexual Arousal Dysfunction**). ICD-11 codes **Sexual Pain-Penetration Disorder** and classifies dysfunctions along etiological/situational dimensions rather than DSM's lifelong/acquired + generalized/situational specifier pairs.

## Assessing Sexual Behavior
Three components (Wiegel, Wincze & Barlow 2002):
1. **Interviews** — supported by questionnaires (patients reveal more on paper). Clinician must appear comfortable, use the patient's **vernacular**, ask about interest, avoidance, fantasies, masturbation, intercourse frequency, abuse/trauma history, arousal, orgasm, and pain; ~2-hour interview also covers relationship + health + comorbidity; partner interviewed concurrently when possible.
2. **Medical examination** — rule out drugs (antihypertensives, anti-anxiety, antidepressants), surgery, diabetes, vascular/neurological disease; urologist (males) / gynecologist (females) check hormones and vascular function.
3. **Psychophysiological assessment** —
   - **Men: penile strain gauge** (Barlow et al. 1970) records penile expansion on a polygraph; rigidity also measured.
   - **Women: vaginal photoplethysmograph** (Geer 1974) — inserted device measures light reflected from vaginal walls (more blood = less light = more arousal).
   - Patients often **unaware** of these objective measures; **self-report frequently diverges** from physiology. Many with psychologically based dysfunction become aroused **in the lab but not with a partner**.

## Causes and Treatment of Sexual Dysfunction
- Multiple dysfunctions usually co-occur in the same patient (e.g., erectile dysfunction + premature ejaculation + low desire together).

### Biological Contributions
- **Prescription drugs** are a major cause: antihypertensive **beta-blockers (propranolol)**; **SSRIs** (e.g., Prozac) and other antidepressants/antianxiety drugs dampen desire/arousal by altering serotonin subtypes. Sexual dysfunction (low desire/arousal) is the **most widespread SSRI side effect** — as many as 80% affected, ~50% a more reliable estimate.
- **Drugs of abuse:** alcohol, cocaine (>60% of cocaine users dysfunctional), heroin. **Alcohol = dual effect** — low doses reduce social inhibition (feel more like sex) but it is a **CNS depressant** that physically suppresses erection/lubrication ("**It provokes the desire, but it takes away the performance**," *Macbeth*). Chronic alcoholism → permanent neurological/liver/testicular damage, lower testosterone, fertility problems; 75% of male alcoholics had dysfunction (Fahrner). **Cigarette smoking** independently linked to erectile dysfunction.
- **Disease:** neurological conditions, **diabetes** (28% of diabetic men had complete erectile failure), kidney disease reduce genital sensitivity. **Vascular disease** is major — erections & vaginal engorgement need blood flow; in men, **arterial insufficiency** (blood can't reach penis) and **venous leakage** (blood flows out too fast). Erectile dysfunction & coronary artery disease coexist → screen ED patients for **cardiovascular disease**. Heart-attack patients may avoid sex from fear of exertion.

### Psychological Contributions (Barlow's model — Figure 10.5)
- Old view: **anxiety** is the sole cause (Masters & Johnson; Kaplan). **Not that simple.**
- Barlow, Sakheim & Beck (1983) shock-threat experiment: in **functional men**, **noncontingent** AND even **contingent shock threat increased arousal** (anxiety can *raise* arousal). Sarrel & Masters (1982): male gang-rape victims achieved erections under threat of harm.
- The real arousal-killer is **distraction**: functional men showed less arousal when distracted by an attended narrative (Abrahamson 1985).
- In **dysfunctional** men, shock-threat anxiety **reduced** arousal (opposite of functional men), but neutral distraction did not.
- Dysfunctional people **underreport** their actual arousal (same strain-gauge level, less reported arousal) — true for women too. **Sad music decreased arousal**; positive mood increased it.
- **Model:** "performance anxiety" = arousal + cognitive processes + **negative affect**.
  - **Functionals (positive feedback loop):** positive affect, attention on **erotic cues**, increasing autonomic arousal → focus even more on erotic cues → functional performance.
  - **Dysfunctionals (negative feedback loop):** negative affect/expectancies, avoid/inaccurately report arousal, perceived lack of control, attention on **public consequences of not performing** → increasing arousal but focused on nonerotic threat → dysfunctional performance.
- **Premature ejaculation:** little known; most prevalent in young men; **excess sympathetic arousal** → lower ejaculation threshold; anxiety further raises sympathetic arousal → vicious cycle.

### Social & Cultural Contributions
- **Erotophobia** (Donn Byrne) — a learned **negative cognitive set** about sex (from family, religion); predicts later sexual difficulty.
- **Early sexual trauma** has long-lasting effects (Laumann 1999): women victimized before puberty ~**2×** more likely to have orgasmic dysfunction; male victims of adult–child contact **>3×** erectile dysfunction; men who sexually assaulted women **3.5×** erectile dysfunction.
- Relationship deterioration, perceiving self or partner as unattractive (Koch 2005), poor sexual skills/communication (Kelly 1990 — anorgasmic women: sex guilt, sex myths, discomfort requesting clitoral stimulation).
- **Script theory** (John Gagnon): we follow social/cultural **"scripts"** guiding sexual behavior; restrictive cultures → more dysfunction. **Vaginismus rare in North America, common in Ireland & Turkey.** India: 77% of male clinic patients had premature-ejaculation complaints, 71% feared nocturnal emissions (cultural belief that **semen loss depletes physical/mental energy**); only 36/1,000 patients female (devaluation of female sexuality).
- **Zilbergeld's male sex myths** — dysfunctional men endorse them more.

### Interaction of Psychological & Physical Factors
- Dysfunction is **almost never purely** psychological or physical. Typical pathway: a myth-believing, anxiety-prone young man has one drug/alcohol-related failure (biological) → anticipates the next encounter anxiously → activates the negative feedback loop (psychological) regardless of substances.

### Treatment
**Education** — surprisingly effective for many (e.g., *Carl*, 55, skipped foreplay → couldn't penetrate; two education sessions fixed it).

**Psychosocial — Masters & Johnson (1970, *Human Sexual Inadequacy*)** revolutionized sex therapy:
- **Male + female co-therapists**, **daily over 2 weeks**, basic education + altering myths + improving communication + eliminating **performance anxiety**.
- **Sensate focus / nondemand pleasuring:** ban intercourse & genital touching first; explore each other's bodies (touch, kiss, massage). Phase 1 = **nongenital pleasuring** (breasts/genitals excluded); phase 2 = **genital pleasuring** (still ban orgasm/intercourse; erection is NOT the goal); then graded penetration (depth/duration built up gradually) → full intercourse.
- **Results:** ~790 patients, recovery in the vast majority; **~100% for premature ejaculation**, **~60%** for lifelong generalized erectile dysfunction.
- Later refinements: **one therapist as good as two**; **weekly as effective as daily**. Masters & Johnson's results exceeded later clinics (possibly higher motivation — patients flew to St. Louis). General sex therapy ED success **60–70%** for several years.
- **Premature ejaculation: squeeze technique** (Semans 1956) — stimulate to near-full erection, partner firmly squeezes where the head meets the shaft → arousal drops; repeat → develops control. 60–90% benefit short-term, drops to ~25% after 3+ years. (Gary treated with this + brief marital therapy.)
- **Lifelong female orgasmic disorder:** explicit **masturbatory training** + disinhibition (Greta used a **vibrator** + talked/shouted aloud, later orgasmed without it). **70–90%** benefit, gains stable/improving.
- **Vaginismus / genito-pelvic pain:** graded insertion of **increasingly larger dilators** at the woman's pace (then partner, then penis), with continued pleasuring + attention to anxiety/abuse memories. **80–100%** overcome vaginismus.
- **Low sexual desire:** reeducation + communication + masturbatory training + erotic exposure; ~**50–70%** benefit initially (Mrs. C: cognitive restructuring + start/stop exercises for her abuse history).

**Medical Treatments** (almost all for **male erectile disorder**):
- **Oral PDE5 drugs:** **sildenafil/Viagra** (1998), **Levitra**, **Cialis**. **50–80%** get erections sufficient for intercourse (vs ~30% placebo). Virag (1999): erections sufficient in 61%, but only **32%** rated results "good"; ~**30% severe headaches** at higher doses; works less well if very anxious; most men **stop within months/a year**. **Adding CBT (Bach 2004)** improved satisfaction. Disappointing in **postmenopausal women** (some benefit only without diminished desire).
- **Testosterone:** safe but **negligible** effect on ED.
- **Injection of vasodilators** (papaverine, prostaglandin) into the penis → erection in 15 min lasting 1–4 hr; somewhat painful → 50–60% discontinue. **MUSE** (papaverine pellet in urethra) less effective, awkward.
- **Penile prostheses/implants** (semirigid rod, or scrotal pump inflating a cylinder, or self-contained inflatable rod) — used **only when other approaches fail**; useful after prostate-cancer removal (newer **nerve-sparing surgery** reduces ED).
- **Vacuum device therapy** — vacuum draws blood in, retained by a ring at the base; **70–100%** satisfactory erections; less intrusive than surgery/injection but awkward.
- **Summary:** combination of psychological + drug treatment when indicated = treatment of choice; treatments for low desire largely untested; sexuality in older adults often ignored.

## Paraphilic Disorders: Clinical Descriptions

- A **paraphilia** = an intense, persistent sexual interest other than in phenotypically normal, mature, **consenting** human partners. It becomes a **paraphilic disorder** only when it **causes distress/impairment** to the individual **or** harm/risk of harm to others. (See DSM Controversies below.)
- Usually **more than one** paraphilic pattern per person (one dominant); high comorbidity with mood, anxiety, substance disorders. Almost exclusively a **male** disorder.
- **Frotteuristic disorder** — arousal from touching/rubbing against a **nonconsenting** person (classic crowded-subway scenario).

> **DSM-5-TR Criteria — Frotteuristic Disorder (Table 10.6):**
> **A.** ≥ **6 months**, recurrent/intense arousal from touching/rubbing against a nonconsenting person (fantasies/urges/behaviors).
> **B.** The person has **acted** on the urges with a nonconsenting person, OR the urges/fantasies cause clinically significant distress/impairment.

### Fetishistic Disorder
- Sexual attraction to **nonliving objects** (women's undergarments, shoes popular) or a **source of tactile stimulation** (rubber, shiny plastic). **Partialism** = attraction to a **body part** (foot, buttocks, hair). Example: stolen bras (fetish); inserting objects into urethra to intensify ejaculation.

> **DSM-5-TR Criteria — Fetishistic Disorder (Table 10.7):**
> **A.** ≥ **6 months**, recurrent/intense arousal from **nonliving objects** OR a highly specific focus on **nongenital body part(s)** (fantasies/urges/behaviors).
> **B.** Causes clinically significant **distress or impairment**.
> **C.** Fetish objects are NOT limited to cross-dressing clothing (→ transvestic disorder) or devices for tactile genital stimulation (e.g., a vibrator).

### Voyeuristic and Exhibitionistic Disorders
- **Voyeuristic disorder** — arousal from observing an **unsuspecting** person undressing/naked/having sex. Many voyeurs don't get the same satisfaction from a strip show — the **risk/anxiety is part of the arousal**. Must be **≥18 years old** to diagnose.
- **Exhibitionistic disorder** — arousal from **exposing genitals** to unsuspecting strangers; behavior must be repeated/compulsive; the thrill of **risk** is central; often (not always) lower education.
- *Cases:* **Robert** (began "peeping" at 14, escalated to exposing to prepubescent girls; arrest *increased* arousal); **The lawyer who needed the bus** (could only be aroused by exposing himself on a partly-full bus, not when his girlfriend role-played it — the real risk was essential).
- **Prevalence (Sweden, n=2,450):** **31%** reported ≥1 incident of being aroused by exposing genitals to a stranger; **7.7%** by spying on others having sex.

> **DSM-5-TR Criteria — Voyeuristic & Exhibitionistic Disorders (Table 10.8):**
> **Voyeuristic:** **A.** ≥ 6 months, arousal from observing an unsuspecting naked/disrobing/sexually-active person. **B.** Acted on urges with a nonconsenting person OR distress/impairment. **C.** Individual is **≥18 years**.
> **Exhibitionistic:** **A.** ≥ 6 months, arousal from exposing genitals to an unsuspecting person. **B.** Acted on urges with a nonconsenting person OR distress/impairment.

### Transvestic Disorder
- Arousal strongly tied to **cross-dressing** (wearing/fantasizing about wearing opposite-sex clothes). Often males in "macho" organizations compensate (e.g., *Mr. M.*, police officer, ex-Marine, 16 years cross-dressing).
- **Prevalence (Sweden):** **2.8% of men, 0.4% of women** reported ≥1 transvestic episode; the **~3% male rate** is generally accepted. **60%** of >1,000 transvestic men were married (Docter & Prince 1997); supportive wives common.
- **Specifiers:** **"with fetishism"** (arousal focused on the clothing itself — indistinguishable from other fetishes) and **"with autogynephilia"** (arousal from **thoughts/images of oneself as a female**, not the clothing). *Case: Ron/Rhonda* — most arousing scenario = imagining himself as a woman doing domestic chores; risky sexual behaviors; controversial overlap with gender dysphoria, but he never sought sex reassignment.

> **DSM-5-TR Criteria — Transvestic Disorder (Table 10.9):**
> **A.** ≥ **6 months**, recurrent/intense arousal from **cross-dressing** (fantasies/urges/behaviors).
> **B.** Causes clinically significant distress/impairment.
> Specify if: **With fetishism** / **With autogynephilia**.

### Sexual Sadism and Sexual Masochism Disorders
- **Sexual sadism** = arousal from **inflicting** physical/psychological pain or humiliation. **Sexual masochism** = arousal from **suffering/receiving** pain or humiliation. Arousal specifically tied to violence/injury.
- **Hypoxiphilia** — self-strangulation to reduce oxygen to the brain and enhance orgasm (distinguished from accidental autoerotic hanging). Behaviors are often mild/harmless but can become dangerous/deadly.
- Mr. M. had **three** deviant patterns simultaneously: masochism, sadism, transvestism. Murderer **Jeffrey Dahmer** = sadism + cannibalism.

> **DSM-5-TR Criteria — Sexual Sadism & Sexual Masochism Disorders (Table 10.10):**
> **Sexual Sadism:** **A.** ≥ 6 months, arousal from the physical/psychological suffering of another (fantasies/urges/behaviors). **B.** Acted on urges with a **nonconsenting** person OR distress/impairment.
> **Sexual Masochism:** **A.** ≥ 6 months, arousal from being humiliated/beaten/bound/made to suffer. **B.** Causes clinically significant distress/impairment.

**Sadistic Rape** — rape is **NOT** classified as a paraphilic disorder; most rapists' arousal is **not** paraphilic. Many meet criteria for **antisocial personality disorder**; rapes are often **opportunistic** (lack of empathy) or **anger/vindictive** (planned). BUT a subgroup of rapists are true **sadists**: Abel et al. (1977) tapes — nonrapists aroused only by **consenting** intercourse; **rapists aroused by both** consenting and forced; some aroused even by **nonsexual aggression** alone. The most brutal patient: raped 100+ times, sadistic fantasies all waking hours → met criteria for **sexual sadism disorder**.

### Pedophilic Disorder and Incest
- **Pedophilia** = sexual attraction to **prepubescent children (generally ≤13)**. ~90% of abusers male, 10% female. As many as **40–50%** of sexual offenders do NOT have pedophilic arousal (their offenses are antisocial/opportunistic). **Being charged with a child-pornography offense is one of the best diagnostic indicators** of pedophilic disorder (Seto 2006).
- **Incest** = pedophilia with **relatives**. Differences: pedophilia victims = young children; incest victims = **girls beginning to mature physically**. Incestuous males are **more aroused by adult women** than pedophiles are (incest = more about availability/family dynamics). *Case: Tony*, incest with daughter from age 12; loved her, bitterly depressed.
- Most child molesters are **not physically abusive** (rationalize as "loving"/"teaching"); rarely consider the psychological damage; children often feel responsible because no force was used.

> **DSM-5-TR Criteria — Pedophilic Disorder (Table 10.11):**
> **A.** ≥ **6 months**, recurrent/intense sexually arousing fantasies, urges, or behaviors involving sexual activity with a **prepubescent child (generally ≤13)**.
> **B.** The person has **acted** on the urges, OR the urges/fantasies cause marked distress/interpersonal difficulty.
> **C.** Individual is **≥16 years** and at least **5 years older** than the child (do NOT include late adolescents in an ongoing relationship with a 12–13-year-old).
> Specify: **Exclusive** (only children) / **Nonexclusive**; attracted to males / females / both; **Limited to incest**.

> **DSM-5-TR note (paraphilias):** DSM-5-TR retains all paraphilic-disorder criteria unchanged from DSM-5. The key DSM-IV → DSM-5 change is the **paraphilia vs paraphilic disorder distinction** (an unusual arousal pattern is only a *disorder* if it causes distress/impairment or harm/risk to others); DSM-5-TR keeps this distinction.

> **Other Specified Paraphilic Disorder (Table 10.12):** symptoms causing distress/impairment that don't meet full criteria for a named disorder. Examples: **telephone scatologia** (obscene calls), **necrophilia** (corpses), **zoophilia** (animals), **coprophilia** (feces), **klismaphilia** (enemas), **urophilia** (urine).

> **ICD-11 note:** ICD-11 renamed the class **"Paraphilic Disorders"** and tightened it around **risk to others / non-consent**: it retains exhibitionistic, voyeuristic, pedophilic (**"Pedophilic Disorder"**), **coercive sexual sadism**, and **frotteuristic** disorders, plus an "other paraphilic disorder involving non-consenting individuals/solitary behavior with marked distress" residual. Critically, ICD-11 **removed Fetishistic Disorder, Fetishistic Transvestism, and (consensual/solitary) Sadomasochism as diagnoses** — consensual or solitary atypical arousal that causes no distress/risk is **not** a disorder, going further than DSM-5 in de-pathologizing harmless variants.

### Paraphilic Disorders in Women
- Long thought absent (except sadomasochism). Now ~**5–10% of sexual offenders are women**. Federoff (1999): 12 cases (5 pedophilia, 4 exhibitionism, 3 sadomasochism). Wiegel (2008): national study of **175 female child sexual abusers**. Cases: woman molesting a 9-year-old while babysitting; woman undressing at window + using animals — both **horrified** and sought treatment.

### Causes of Paraphilic Disorders (model — Figure 10.6)
- **Early inappropriate sexual associations/experiences** (some accidental, some vicarious — e.g., Tony's uncle modeling fondling; Robert's first erotic experience while "peeping").
- → possible **inadequate development of consensual adult arousal** + **inadequate adult social skills** (Marshall). Many with poor skills don't develop paraphilias, though.
- **Inappropriate sexual fantasies repeatedly paired with masturbation and strongly reinforced** (Rachman & Hodgson conditioned arousal to a boot). Operant conditioning: arousal → orgasm reinforcement, fantasized thousands of times before acting. Explains why paraphilias are **almost exclusively male** (men masturbate far more across cultures).
- **Repeated attempts to inhibit** undesired arousal **paradoxically increase** the thoughts/fantasies (like OCD thought suppression; possible weak **behavioral inhibition system [BIS]** / serotonergic dysfunction; very high sex drive — one rapist masturbated every 30 min all day).
- Counterpoint: **Sambia** boys have homosexual childhood but become heterosexual adults → strong cultural **scripts** can override early experience. Biological dimension not yet in the model.

## Assessing and Treating Paraphilic Disorders
- Assessment uses the **penile strain gauge** and the model above (assess paraphilic arousal + desired adult arousal + social skills + relationship ability). Sometimes the patient doesn't know the true cue (the "white sandals" man was actually aroused by **feet**, not sandals).

### Psychological Treatment (mostly behavior therapy)
- **Covert sensitization** (Cautela 1967) — in imagination, pair arousing images with **aversive/dangerous consequences**. *Tony's scene:* caught with his daughter by his wife and the family priest → shame, loss of love/respect. Over 6–8 sessions narrated dramatically, imagined daily until arousal disappears. Tony's incestuous arousal eliminated in 3–4 weeks (confirmed psychophysiologically); did NOT reduce healthy fatherly desire; relapse at 3-mo follow-up tracked to marital decline → **marital therapy restored gains** (Figure 10.7, card-sort scores).
- **Orgasmic reconditioning** (Davison 1968) — masturbate to usual fantasy but switch to a **desirable** fantasy just before ejaculation, then move it progressively earlier → strengthens appropriate arousal.
- **Relapse prevention** — recognize early temptation signs, apply self-control before urges intensify.
- **Family/systems intervention** usually needed.

### Outcomes
- **Mixed evidence.** Incarcerated/severe offenders: modest at best; large California study showed little effect over 8-year follow-up. Several surveys: psychological treatment reduces sexual **recidivism** by **11–20%** over usual care; **CBT most effective**.
- **Outpatients** (less severe): better. **Maletzky (2002):** >8,000 offenders over 20 years, strict 4-part success criterion (completed treatment, no deviant arousal on strain gauge at follow-up, no reported deviant arousal, no legal charges) → **75–95% success** depending on offense type. **Rapists lowest (75%)**; **multiple paraphilias lowest overall**. Failure predictors: unstable relationships, unstable employment, denial, multiple victims, living with a victim.
- **Becker** adolescent offender program: only **10%** reoffended. **Carpentier (2006):** CBT for aggressive/inappropriate sexual behavior in children 5–12 → only **2%** later offended (vs play therapy) — promising **prevention**.

### Drug Treatments
- **Cyproterone acetate** — antiandrogen ("chemical castration"); dramatically lowers testosterone, eliminating desire/fantasy (returns when stopped).
- **Medroxyprogesterone (Depo-Provera injectable)** — hormonal agent reducing testosterone.
- **Triptorelin** (inhibits gonadotropin) — Rösler & Witztum (1998): successful "chemical castration" of 30 men with severe paraphilias; possibly more effective with fewer side effects (one study).
- Used mainly for **dangerous offenders unresponsive** to other treatment (only 8 of ~5,000 Maletzky patients needed drugs). Effective only if taken regularly; prison-as-alternative motivates compliance.
- **Summary:** outlook for most is **bleak** — paraphilic disorders run a **chronic course** with common recurrence; psychosocial treatment scarce outside specialized centers.

> **DSM Controversies — Paraphilia vs Paraphilic Disorder:** DSM-IV diagnosed a paraphilia on the presence of intense/persistent interest + clinically significant distress/impairment. **DSM-5 split** the two: a **paraphilia** is any intense/persistent atypical interest; a **paraphilic disorder** additionally causes distress/impairment to self OR harm/risk to others. So a strong arousal to children that is **not acted on and causes no distress** is, by definition, **not a disorder** (abnormal but like being unusually tall). Change made to accommodate the high prevalence of occasional "paraphilic" fantasies in people without disorders (Ahlers 2011). **Critics:** anyone with such strong, persistent arousal would likely at least watch a video online → harm occurs anyway.

## Gender Dysphoria

### Defining Gender Dysphoria
- **Gender identity** = deep-seated personal sense of the gender you actually experience (more than anatomy, arousal, or family/society reactions).
- **Gender dysphoria** = physical/**natal** sex is **incongruent** with experienced gender + distress. On a **continuum**; at the extreme, individuals **reject** their natal sex and wish to change it ("a woman trapped in a man's body"). *Case: Joe*, 17, thought of himself as a girl since memory, cross-dressed from age 5, ran away, attempted suicide, wanted surgery to become a woman.
- Terminology: natal female + male identity = **transsexual man / "transman"**; natal male + female identity = **transwoman**. **"Posttransition"** = living full-time in the experienced gender and preparing for / having had sex reassignment surgery (a diagnostic specifier).

**Distinguish from:**
- **Transvestic disorder** — there the primary purpose of cross-dressing is **sexual gratification**; in gender dysphoria the goal is to **live as the other gender**, not arousal.
- **Disorders of Sex Development (DSD)** — formerly "intersexuality"/"hermaphroditism"; ambiguous genitalia + documented hormonal/physical abnormality; usually "assigned" a sex at birth ± surgery/hormones. Gender dysphoria can occur with a DSD (specified if so), but **most** with gender dysphoria have **no physical abnormality**.
- **Same-sex arousal patterns** — an effeminate gay man or masculine lesbian does NOT feel trapped in the wrong body. **Gender identity is independent of sexual arousal pattern** — e.g., a transwoman may be attracted to women; heterosexual women before surgery may be **gay men after** surgery (Coleman 1993).
- **Autogynephilia** — a distinct developmental subset where gender dysphoria begins with a **specific sexual attraction to a fantasy of oneself as a female (auto-gyne)** that progresses into an all-encompassing experienced female gender. These natal males were **not effeminate as boys** but became aroused cross-dressing/imagining themselves as women (Blanchard; Bailey 2003). Lawrence (2005): 54% of 232 transwomen were heterosexual before surgery, only 25% after → some become "technically gay." **Controversial but research-supported.**

> **DSM-5-TR Criteria — Gender Dysphoria (Table 10.13):**
> **In Children:** **A.** Marked incongruence between experienced/expressed gender and assigned gender, ≥ **6 months**, ≥ **6 of 8** (must include A1): (1) strong desire to be / insistence one **is** the other gender; (2) strong preference for cross-dressing; (3) preference for cross-gender make-believe roles; (4) preference for other-gender toys/games/activities; (5) preference for other-gender playmates; (6) strong rejection of typical same-gender toys/play; (7) strong dislike of one's sexual anatomy; (8) strong desire for sex characteristics matching experienced gender. **B.** Clinically significant distress/impairment.
> **In Adolescents/Adults:** **A.** Marked incongruence ≥ 6 months, ≥ **2 of 6**: (1) incongruence with primary/secondary sex characteristics; (2) desire to be rid of them; (3) desire for the other gender's sex characteristics; (4) desire to **be** the other gender; (5) desire to be **treated** as the other gender; (6) conviction one has the typical feelings/reactions of the other gender. **B.** Clinically significant distress/impairment.
> Specify: **With a disorder of sex development**; **Posttransition**.

> **DSM-5-TR note (gender dysphoria):** DSM-5 renamed DSM-IV's **"Gender Identity Disorder" → "Gender Dysphoria"** to reduce stigma (the *distress*, not the identity, is the disorder) and moved it to its own chapter. **DSM-5-TR (2022)** kept the diagnosis but **revised the wording** to be more affirming: "**individuals assigned male/female at birth**" (AMAB/AFAB) replaces "natal male/female"; "**desired gender**" and "another gender" replace "the other gender"; "**gender-affirming**" replaces "cross-sex"/"sex reassignment." The criteria structure (children vs adolescents/adults, ≥6 months, the 6-of-8 / 2-of-6 counts) is unchanged.

> **ICD-11 note:** ICD-11 made the most significant reclassification of all: it **removed gender incongruence from the mental-disorders chapter entirely** and created **"Gender Incongruence"** in a new chapter on **"Conditions Related to Sexual Health."** It also **dropped the distress requirement** (incongruence itself, not distress, is coded), to **de-psychopathologize** trans identities while preserving access to care. ICD-11 has **Gender Incongruence of Adolescence and Adulthood** and **of Childhood**. This is a current-classification contrast with DSM-5-TR, which retains it as a (renamed, distress-based) mental-disorder diagnosis.

### Causes
- **Genetics:** Coolidge (2002) twin study estimated **~62%** of vulnerability to gender dysphoria is genetic, 38% nonshared environment. Netherlands twin registry: **70%** of cross-gender *behavior* genetic (but that ≠ gender identity, which wasn't measured). MZ twins discordant for gender dysphoria exist (Segal). Genetics clearly part of the picture but no specific contribution identified.
- **Prenatal hormones:** higher testosterone/estrogen at critical periods might masculinize/feminize the fetus. **Congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH):** chromosomal females flooded with androgens → masculine genitalia & **masculine behavior**, but **no difference in gender identity** (Meyer-Bahlburg 2004) → no established link between prenatal hormones and later gender identity. Structural brain differences (more "feminine" brain region) seen in male-to-female gender dysphoria (Zhou 1995) — cause or effect unclear.
- **Timing:** gender identity firms up between **18 months and 3 years** and is relatively fixed after.
- **Bruce/Brenda (David Reimer):** male twin's penis destroyed during circumcision → reassigned and raised as a girl "Brenda" but **never adjusted** (preferred rough play, stood to urinate, suicidal at 14); told the truth → reverted to Bruce/David, married, but **committed suicide at 38** (2004). Powerful evidence **biology expresses itself** despite rearing.
- **Gender nonconformity** (Richard Green) — feminine boys / masculine girls. Most "feminine" behavior in boys is discouraged and ceases; persistent boys sometimes encouraged. Possible factors: excessive maternal attention/contact, lack of male playmates, higher fetal testosterone. **Follow-up:** few gender-nonconforming boys develop gender incongruence; the most likely outcome is **homosexual orientation (~40%)**, 32% bisexual, **60% heterosexual**. Girls (Drummond 2008): of 25 followed from ~age 9 with childhood gender dysphoria, only **3 met criteria** as adults; 6 bi/homosexual behavior; 8 homosexual fantasy only; 8 heterosexual. Loose relationship is cross-cultural (Samoan **Fa'afafine**; strict Muslim societies). Causes still largely a **mystery**.

### Treatment

**Sex Reassignment Surgery** (adults; APA guideline Byne 2012 — least-intrusive-first sequence):
1. Full psychological evaluation + education.
2. **Partially reversible:** gonadal hormones for desired secondary sex characteristics.
3. **Nonreversible:** sex reassignment surgery.
- Must **live in the desired gender 1–2 years** + be psychologically/financially/socially stable.
- **Transwomen:** hormones for breast growth (gynecomastia) + secondary characteristics; electrolysis for facial hair; genitals removed, vagina constructed.
- **Transmen:** artificial penis from skin/muscle grafts (more complex), breasts removed.
- **Satisfaction:** predominantly successful (**75–100% satisfied**; recent averages ~**90%**); **transmen adjust better** than transwomen. But **1–7% regret** the (irreversible) surgery; as many as **2% attempt suicide after** surgery (higher than general population). Regret predictors: **misdiagnosis**, comorbid alcohol use/psychosis, poor family support. Assessments are complex → only at **highly specialized gender clinics** (Campo 2003: 186 Dutch psychiatrists showed little diagnostic consensus, decisions rested on personal preference). *Renee Richards / Richard Raskin* example.

**Treatment of Gender Nonconformity in Children** (highly controversial; APA guideline Byne 2012 lists three options, little hard evidence):
1. **Reduce** gender dysphoria & cross-gender behavior (assumes it won't persist; avoids social rejection + later surgery).
2. **"Watchful waiting"** — let gender unfold naturally (needs strong caregiver/community support).
3. **Actively affirm/encourage** cross-gender identification (critics: nonconformity usually doesn't persist; affirmation may increase persistence).
- Society's dilemma: free expression vs difficult social adaptation, given nonconformity usually does NOT persist (Skidmore 2006: nonconformity linked to distress in gay men, not lesbians).

**Treatment of DSD (Intersexuality):** standard = surgery + hormones soon after birth to match assigned gender, but gender dysphoria may still develop later (→ same least-intrusive sequence). **Anne Fausto-Sterling** proposed **five sexes** (males, females, **herms** [true hermaphrodites], **merms** [more male + some female genitalia], **ferms** [ovaries + some male genitalia]); ~**1.7% (17/1,000)** born with some DSD. Many are dissatisfied with surgery (like Bruce) → growing push to **delay early irreversible genital surgery** until the psychological gender identity is clear, using psychological support meanwhile.

## High-Yield Exam Recap
- **Three separate DSM-5 classes:** sexual dysfunctions, paraphilic disorders, gender dysphoria (the last is NOT a sexual disorder).
- **Key DSM-5 mergers:** female desire + arousal → **Female Sexual Interest/Arousal Disorder**; vaginismus + dyspareunia → **Genito-Pelvic Pain/Penetration Disorder**.
- **All dysfunction criteria:** ~6-month duration + clinically significant distress + exclusions; specify lifelong/acquired, generalized/situational.
- **Premature ejaculation** = most common male dysfunction (21%), defined ~1 min; **female orgasmic disorder** = most common female complaint; **erectile dysfunction** = most common male presenting complaint, rises steeply with age.
- **Barlow's model:** anxiety can *increase* arousal in functionals; **distraction + negative affect + attention on nonerotic threat** drive dysfunction.
- **Masters & Johnson:** sensate focus / nondemand pleasuring; squeeze technique (PE); dilators (vaginismus); masturbatory training + vibrator (anorgasmia). **Viagra/PDE5** for ED.
- **Paraphilia vs paraphilic disorder** distinction (DSM-5) — disorder only if distress/impairment or harm/risk to others. Almost exclusively male; conditioned via fantasy + masturbation reinforcement.
- **Pedophilic disorder:** ≥6 mo, prepubescent child (≤13), individual ≥16 and ≥5 years older. Rape is NOT a paraphilic disorder. Treatment: covert sensitization, orgasmic reconditioning, relapse prevention, CBT; **chemical castration** (cyproterone, MPA/Depo-Provera, triptorelin) for severe cases. Chronic, recurrence common.
- **Gender dysphoria:** identity independent of arousal; firms up 18 mo–3 yr; **Bruce/Brenda/David Reimer** = biology over rearing; treatment = staged least-intrusive (eval → hormones → surgery), ~90% satisfaction, transmen adjust better; **ICD-11 moved it out of mental disorders** ("Gender Incongruence," no distress requirement).
