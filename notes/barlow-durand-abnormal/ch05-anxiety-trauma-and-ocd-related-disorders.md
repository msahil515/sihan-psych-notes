# Ch 5: Anxiety, Trauma- and Stressor-Related, and Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorders

This chapter covers the full anxiety spectrum and the two new DSM-5 sibling classes that were split out of "anxiety disorders": **trauma- and stressor-related disorders** (PTSD, acute stress, adjustment, attachment disorders) and **obsessive-compulsive and related disorders** (OCD, BDD, hoarding, trichotillomania, excoriation). It is one of the highest-yield exam chapters: it defines the core emotions (anxiety vs fear vs panic), gives Barlow's **triple vulnerability theory** that recurs as the etiological backbone of every disorder, and pairs each disorder's clinical description, statistics, causes, and evidence-based treatments. Master the diagnostic criteria sets and the integrated bio-psycho-social causal models.

> DSM-5-TR note (chapter-wide): The book is written to DSM-5 (2013). DSM-5-TR (2022) kept all of these disorders in the same three chapters with the same core criteria. Main TR-era updates relevant here: updated ICD-10-CM codes, addition of explicit **suicidal behavior / nonsuicidal self-injury** coding, clarified wording on culture and on symptom thresholds, and (in the trauma chapter) **Prolonged Grief Disorder** added as a new diagnosis (covered with depressive/grief material, not in this chapter's body). No anxiety/OCD diagnostic criterion in this chapter was substantively changed by TR.

## The Complexity of Anxiety Disorders

### Anxiety, Fear, and Panic: definitions
- **Anxiety** = a negative mood state characterized by **bodily symptoms of physical tension** and **apprehension about the future** (APA, 2013; Barlow, 2002). It is **future-oriented**; subjective unease + worried behavior + physiological arousal (elevated HR, muscle tension).
- Anxiety is **good in moderation** (Yerkes & Dodson, 1908: we perform better when slightly anxious). Howard Liddell (1949) called anxiety the **"shadow of intelligence."** Too much anxiety impairs performance.
- Anxiety is closely related to **depression** (relevant to Ch 7).
- **Fear** = an **immediate alarm reaction to present danger**; activates massive **autonomic (sympathetic) nervous system** response, driving the **flight-or-fight response**. Present-oriented; strong escapist action tendencies.
- **Panic attack** = an **abrupt experience of intense fear or acute discomfort** accompanied by physical symptoms (heart palpitations, chest pain, shortness of breath, dizziness). Named after the Greek god **Pan**. It is "fear occurring at an inappropriate time," when there is nothing to be afraid of.

> **DSM-5-TR criteria — Panic Attack** (a *specifier*, not a disorder): An abrupt surge of intense fear/discomfort peaking within minutes, with **4 or more** of 13 symptoms: 1) palpitations/pounding/accelerated HR, 2) sweating, 3) trembling/shaking, 4) shortness of breath/smothering, 5) feeling of choking, 6) chest pain/discomfort, 7) nausea/abdominal distress, 8) dizzy/unsteady/lightheaded/faint, 9) chills/heat sensations, 10) paresthesias (numbness/tingling), 11) **derealization** (unreality) or **depersonalization** (detached from oneself), 12) fear of losing control / going crazy, 13) fear of dying.

- **Two types of panic attacks** (DSM-5): **expected (cued)** — tied to a known situation (common in specific & social phobia); **unexpected (uncued)** — no warning (central to **panic disorder**).
- Case **Gretchen** ("Attacked by Panic"): first attack at 25 after appendectomy; woke at night with pounding heart, chest pain, felt detached/robotic, thought she was dying. **Nocturnal** panic.
- A recorded lab panic attack: HR surged minutes 11-13 with rising frontalis EMG and finger temperature; **peaked and subsided within ~3 minutes**.

### Causes of Anxiety and Related Disorders
- **Biological contributions:** we **inherit a tendency to be tense/anxious**; tendency to panic also runs in families (genetic component partly distinct from anxiety). **No single gene**; collections of genes across chromosomes create vulnerability that stress "turns on."
  - **Neurotransmitters/systems:** depleted **GABA** (GABA–benzodiazepine system) → ↑ anxiety; **noradrenergic** and **serotonergic** systems implicated; growing focus on the **corticotropin-releasing factor (CRF) system**, which activates the **HPA axis** and affects the **limbic system** (hippocampus, amygdala), locus coeruleus, prefrontal cortex, dopaminergic system.
  - **Limbic system** = brain area most associated with anxiety (mediator between brain stem and cortex).
  - **Behavioral Inhibition System (BIS)** — Jeffrey Gray: septal–hippocampal circuit to frontal cortex; activated by unexpected danger signals + boost from amygdala → freeze, anxiety, apprehensive scanning.
  - **Fight/Flight System (FFS)** — Gray & Graeff: brain stem through amygdala, ventromedial hypothalamus, central gray; produces immediate alarm/escape (looks like panic); activated partly by **serotonin deficiency**. **BIS ≠ FFS** circuit.
  - Environment can change circuit sensitivity: teen **smoking 20+ cigarettes/day** → **15× more likely** to develop panic disorder, **5× more likely** GAD (Johnson et al., 2000).
  - Brain imaging: in anxiety disorders the **amygdala is overly responsive** (abnormal **bottom-up** processing) while cortical down-regulation is **deficient** (abnormal **top-down** processing).
- **Psychological contributions:** Freud — anxiety = psychic reaction to danger; behaviorists — classical conditioning/modeling. Integrated model: early **sense of uncontrollability** develops from upbringing; **overprotective/overintrusive parents** who "clear the way" prevent children from learning control. Panic explained by **conditioning + cognitive** processes: a **true alarm** becomes a **learned (false) alarm** conditioned to external cues (places) and **internal cues** (↑HR, respiration). Cues can travel **eye → amygdala** bypassing cortex (unconscious).
- **Social contributions:** stressful life events (marriage, divorce, work, death, school) trigger vulnerabilities. Stress reaction patterns **run in families**.

### Triple Vulnerability Theory (Barlow) — central exam concept
1. **Generalized biological vulnerability** — heritable tendency to be uptight/high-strung (negative affect). Not sufficient alone.
2. **Generalized psychological vulnerability** — belief from early experience that the world is dangerous/uncontrollable and one cannot cope.
3. **Specific psychological vulnerability** — learning (e.g., from parents) that *particular* objects/situations or sensations are dangerous (e.g., parent fears dogs → child fears dogs; "physical sensations are dangerous").
- All three + a stressor greatly increase odds of developing an anxiety disorder.

### Comorbidity, physical disorders, suicide
- High comorbidity: in 1,127 patients, **55%** had ≥1 additional anxiety/depressive disorder at assessment; **76%** at some point in life. **Major depression** = most common additional diagnosis (**50%** lifetime). Comorbid depression/substance abuse → ↓ recovery, ↑ relapse.
- **Comorbidity with physical disorders:** any anxiety disorder uniquely associated with **thyroid, respiratory, GI, arthritis, migraine, allergic** conditions (Sareen et al., 2006); anxiety usually **precedes** the physical disorder; worse disability/QoL if both present. Also cardiovascular disease (esp. panic). DSM-5 notes panic attacks co-occur with cardio/respiratory/GI/**vestibular (inner ear)** conditions.
- **Suicide:** **20%** of panic disorder patients had attempted suicide (risk comparable to major depression). Any anxiety/related disorder uniquely ↑ suicidal ideation/attempts; strongest with **panic disorder and PTSD**. Anxiety + depression → much higher risk than depression alone.

> ICD-11 note: ICD-11 groups Anxiety and Fear-Related Disorders together (incl. separation anxiety and selective mutism in this block, as DSM-5 also does) but places **OCD and related disorders** and **disorders specifically associated with stress (PTSD, Complex PTSD, prolonged grief, adjustment disorder)** in their own separate blocks — broadly parallel to DSM-5's three-chapter split.

## ANXIETY DISORDERS

> DSM-5 added two disorders to the anxiety class: **separation anxiety disorder** and **selective mutism** (both previously in childhood-disorders sections).

### Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD)
- **Clinical description:** ≥6 months of **excessive anxiety and worry** (apprehensive expectation), more days than not, that is **difficult to control**. Worry about **minor, everyday events** (distinguishing feature: **100%** of GAD patients worry excessively about minor things vs ~50% of other anxiety disorders). Physical profile differs from panic: **muscle tension**, mental agitation, fatigue, irritability, sleep disturbance (autonomic *restrictors*, not surgers).
  - Case **Irene** ("Ruled by Worry"): 3.7 GPA but convinced she'd flunk; worried about grades, relationships, health; chronic catastrophizing.

> **DSM-5-TR criteria — GAD:** A) excessive anxiety/worry more days than not for ≥6 months about several events/activities; B) difficult to control the worry; C) associated with **≥3 of 6** symptoms (only **1** required in children): restlessness/keyed up, easily fatigued, difficulty concentrating/mind blank, irritability, muscle tension, sleep disturbance; D) clinically significant distress/impairment; E) not due to substance/medical condition; F) not better explained by another disorder.

- **Statistics:** 1-yr prevalence **3.1%**, lifetime **5.7%**; adolescents 1-yr **1.1%**. **~2/3 female** (sex ratio may be developed-country specific — male-predominant in rural South Africa). Median onset age **31**; earlier/more gradual onset than other anxiety disorders; **chronic** course (only 58% recovered after 12 yrs; 45% of those relapsed). High prevalence in **older adults** (up to 10%); high tranquilizer use in elderly (17-50%).
- **Causes:** generalized biological vulnerability (inherit *tendency to be anxious*, not GAD itself). GAD patients are **autonomic restrictors** (less physiological responsivity); the one measure that distinguishes them = chronic **muscle tension**. Highly **sensitive to threat** (Stroop: slower to color-name threat words even at 20 ms). Borkovec: intense left-frontal **cognitive worry without imagery** → avoids the affective/imaginal processing → worry acts as **avoidance** (like phobic avoidance), preventing emotional processing and adaptation.
- **Treatment:** **Benzodiazepines** give modest short-term relief but impair cognition/motor function, cause **dependence**, ↑ falls/hip fractures in elderly (best for short-term crisis use, ≤1-2 weeks). Stronger evidence for **antidepressants** (paroxetine/Paxil, venlafaxine/Effexor). **CBT** (evoke worry, confront images, cognitive coping) = about equal short-term, **better long-term**. Newer **acceptance/mindfulness-based** approaches (Roemer & Orsillo). In children: CBT and sertraline (Zoloft) each effective; **combination best** (80% improved vs 24% placebo; Walkup et al., 2008).

### Panic Disorder (PD) and Agoraphobia
- **PD** = recurrent **unexpected** panic attacks + ≥1 month of worry about further attacks/their consequences or maladaptive behavior change. **Agoraphobia** = fear/avoidance of situations where escape is difficult or help unavailable if panic-like symptoms occur.
  - DSM-IV had "panic disorder with agoraphobia"; **DSM-5 separated them** (each can occur without the other; ~50% of agoraphobia in surveys has **no** panic history). Case **Mrs. M.** ("Self-Imprisoned"): housebound 20 years.
- **Interoceptive avoidance** = avoiding internal physical sensations (e.g., exercise, saunas, caffeine, sex) that mimic panic onset. Agoraphobic avoidance is driven by **expectation** of another attack, not number/severity; can persist independent of attacks. Coping also via **drugs/alcohol**.

> **DSM-5-TR criteria — Panic Disorder:** A) recurrent unexpected panic attacks; B) ≥1 attack followed by ≥1 month of (a) persistent concern/worry about more attacks or consequences AND/OR (b) significant maladaptive behavior change (e.g., avoiding exercise/unfamiliar situations); C) not attributable to substance/medical condition; D) not better explained by another disorder.

> **DSM-5-TR criteria — Agoraphobia:** A) marked fear/anxiety about **≥2 of 5**: public transportation, open spaces, enclosed places, standing in line/crowds, being outside the home alone; B) fears these because escape might be hard / help unavailable if panic-like or incapacitating/embarrassing symptoms occur; C) almost always provoke fear; D) avoided/require companion/endured with intense fear; E) out of proportion to actual danger; F) persistent, **typically ≥6 months**; G) clinically significant impairment; H–I) excessive even if a medical condition is present, not better explained by another disorder.

- **Statistics:** PD 1-yr **2.7%**, lifetime **4.7%** (2/3 women). Agoraphobia without panic ~1.4% lifetime. Onset early adult life, median **20-24**; rare prepubertal. **75%+ of severe agoraphobia is female** (cultural explanation: acceptable for women to report fear/avoid; men "tough it out" or **abuse alcohol** → masked PD).
- **Cultural influences:** PD worldwide. **Susto** (Latin America — fright disorder, somatic, no reported fear). **Ataques de nervios** (Hispanic/Caribbean — panic-like + shouting/crying). **Kyol goeu / "wind overload"** (Khmer/Cambodian refugees, with orthostatic dizziness and "sore neck"). Somatic symptoms emphasized in many non-Western cultures.
- **Nocturnal panic:** ~**60%** of PD patients; attacks most often **1:30-3:30 a.m.**, during **delta (slow-wave)** sleep — **NOT** REM, so **not nightmares** (rules out "repressed dream material"). Distinguish from **sleep apnea**, **sleep terrors** (children, stage 4, no memory), and **isolated sleep paralysis** ("the witch is riding you," culturally determined, higher in African Americans with PD — 59.6% — REM intruding into waking).
- **Causes:** triple vulnerability. **8-12%** of population has an occasional unexpected attack; only ~5% develop PD (those with the **specific psychological vulnerability** that unexplained bodily sensations are dangerous). **David Clark's cognitive theory:** catastrophic misinterpretation of normal physical sensations → anxiety → more sensations → vicious cycle. Little support for psychodynamic separation-anxiety origin.
- **Treatment:**
  - **Medication:** SSRIs (Prozac, Paxil) = indicated drug (but sexual dysfunction in 75%+); SNRIs (venlafaxine); high-potency **benzodiazepines (alprazolam/Xanax)** work fast but dependence/relapse (~90% relapse off benzos). ~60% panic-free while on drug; ~50% relapse off SSRIs.
  - **Psychological:** exposure-based treatment for agoraphobia; **Panic Control Treatment (PCT)** (Barlow/Craske) = **interoceptive exposure** (induce "mini" panics by exercise/spinning) + cognitive therapy. ~70% improve; gains last ≥2 yrs. **Booster sessions** lower relapse (5.2% vs 18.4%).
  - **Combined:** NIMH study (Barlow, Gorman, Shear, Woods, 2000) — CBT, imipramine, CBT+IMI, placebo, placebo+CBT. All beat placebo; **combined no better** than individual; **CBT alone retained gains best** after drug stopped. **Stepped-care** approach favored: psychological treatment first, add drugs only if needed. "Calm Tools for Living" computer-guided program for primary care.

### Specific Phobia
- **Definition:** irrational fear of a specific object/situation markedly interfering with functioning. Fear out of proportion to actual danger; situation actively avoided.

> **DSM-5-TR criteria — Specific Phobia:** A) marked fear/anxiety about a specific object/situation; B) almost always provokes immediate fear (children: crying, tantrums, freezing, clinging); C) actively avoided/endured with intense fear; D) out of proportion to actual danger; E) **persistent, typically ≥6 months**; F) clinically significant impairment; G) not better explained by another disorder. **Specify type:** 1) Animal, 2) Natural environment (heights, storms, water), 3) Blood-injection-injury, 4) Situational (planes, elevators, enclosed places), 5) Other (choking, vomiting, illness; in children loud sounds/costumed characters).

- **Subtypes & onset ages:**
  - **Blood-injection-injury phobia:** unique **vasovagal** response — HR/BP **drop**, fainting (opposite of usual sympathetic surge). **Runs in families most strongly** of any phobia. Onset ~**9 yrs**.
  - **Situational phobia** (claustrophobia, flying): onset mid-teens to mid-20s; unlike PD, **no panic outside** the phobic situation. ~30% of first-degree relatives affected.
  - **Natural environment phobia** (heights, storms, water): cluster together; onset ~**7 yrs**; some "preparedness."
  - **Animal phobia:** onset ~**7 yrs**.
- **Statistics:** lifetime ~**12.5%**; 1-yr **8.7%** (adolescents 15.8%) — one of the most common disorders. Sex ratio ~**4:1 female**. Median onset **7 yrs** (youngest except separation anxiety). Chronic. Hispanics 2× more likely to report. **Pa-leng / frigophobia** (Chinese — "fear of the cold," yin-yang imbalance).
- **Causes — four pathways:** 1) **direct experience** (true alarm, e.g., dog bite); 2) experiencing a **false alarm** (unexpected panic) in a situation (Munjack: ~50% of driving phobics never had a real accident); 3) **vicarious experience** (observing another's fear — Öst's dental-drill case); 4) **information transmission** (being told/warned repeatedly — Öst's snake-phobia woman). **Preparedness**: inherited tendency to fear evolutionarily dangerous things. Must also develop anxiety about recurrence (generalized psych vulnerability — DiNardo: 50% of *non*-phobics also had a scary dog encounter but didn't develop anxiety). Heritability: ~28-31% of first-degree relatives have a phobia (vs ~10% controls); subtypes "breed true." Cultural: males underreport.
- **Treatment:** **structured, consistent exposure-based exercises** (under supervision). Many phobias (incl. blood phobia) treatable in a **single session of 2-6 hours** (Öst). For blood phobia: **applied tension** (tense muscle groups to keep BP up and prevent fainting). Treatment "rewires" the brain (↓ amygdala/insula/cingulate reactivity, ↑ prefrontal control).

### Separation Anxiety Disorder
- Children's **unrealistic, persistent worry** that harm will befall attachment figures or themselves, separating them from loved ones (lost, kidnapped, killed). School refusal (fear of separation, **not** school itself — distinguish from school phobia). **4.1%** of children; if untreated extends into adulthood in ~35%; lifetime adult prevalence ~**6.6%**.
- **Now in DSM-5 it is a full diagnostic category** (elevated from childhood section) and recognized across the lifespan. Treatment: parent-involved exposure; intensive programs.

### Social Anxiety Disorder (SAD / Social Phobia)
- Marked fear/anxiety about social or performance situations involving possible **scrutiny/negative evaluation**. More than shyness. **Performance-only** subtype (e.g., public speaking, "stage fright," **paruresis**/"bashful bladder"). Cases: **Billy** (extreme shyness), **Steve Sax / Chuck Knoblauch** (throwing yips), Scarlett Johansson, Ricky Williams.

> **DSM-5-TR criteria — SAD:** A) marked fear/anxiety about ≥1 social situation with possible scrutiny (conversation, being observed, performing); B) fears acting/showing anxiety that will be negatively evaluated; C) almost always provokes fear (children: crying/tantrums/freezing/clinging/mutism); D) avoided/endured with intense fear; E) out of proportion; F) **persistent, typically ≥6 months**; G) impairment; H-J) not due to substance/medical/another disorder. **Specify: Performance only.**

- **Statistics:** lifetime **12.1%**, 1-yr **6.8%** (adolescents 8.2%) — **second most prevalent** anxiety disorder after specific phobia. **Sex ratio ~50:50** (unlike female-predominant others). Onset adolescence, peak **~13 yrs**. Asian cultures lowest rates; Russian/US highest. **Taijin kyofusho** (Japan; subtype of shinkeishitsu) — fear of **offending/embarrassing others** (eye contact, body odor, blushing); 3:2 male; related "olfactory reference syndrome" resembles OCD.
- **Causes:** evolutionary preparedness to fear **angry/critical/rejecting faces** (learned faster, extinguished slower; eye region most threatening; ↑ amygdala, ↓ cortical control). **Kagan**: inherited temperament of **behavioral inhibition** evident by **4 months** → risk for phobic behavior. Three pathways (parallel to other phobias): generalized bio/psych vulnerability + (1) stress-disrupted performance without alarm, (2) unexpected panic in social situation, or (3) real social trauma (**92%** of social phobics severely teased/bullied as children vs 35-50% of other anxiety disorders). Plus specific psychological vulnerability (social evaluation is dangerous; parents model social fear; relatives' risk 16% vs 5%).
- **Treatment:** **Clark's cognitive therapy** (real-life experiences to disprove danger; 84% benefit, superior to IPT). Hofmann's treatment targets avoidance/safety behaviors via **social mishap exposures**. Adolescent CBT (family-based outperforms individual when parents also anxious). **Drugs:** beta-blockers **not** supported; SSRIs (Paxil, Zoloft, Effexor) FDA-approved. CT > Prozac in head-to-head (Clark et al., 2003). **D-cycloserine (DCS)** given ~1 hr before exposure speeds extinction via glutamate system in amygdala (24-33% faster improvement; initiates effect sooner but doesn't augment full course).

### Selective Mutism (SM)
- Now grouped with anxiety disorders in DSM-5. Rare childhood disorder: **lack of speech in settings where speaking is expected** (e.g., school) despite speaking elsewhere (e.g., home); not due to lack of language/physical difficulty/autism. Must last **>1 month** (not just first month of school). Strongly tied to social anxiety (nearly 100% comorbid SAD in one study). Prevalence ~**0.5%**; girls > boys. Treatment: CBT principles (modeling, stimulus fading, shaping) — e.g., "Brave Buddies Camp."

## TRAUMA- AND STRESSOR-RELATED DISORDERS

> New DSM-5 chapter consolidating disorders that follow a **proximal stressful/traumatic event** with intense emotional responses (a wider range than fear: rage, horror, guilt, shame). Includes attachment disorders, adjustment disorders, acute stress disorder, PTSD.

### Posttraumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)
- **Setting event:** exposure to actual/threatened **death, serious injury, or sexual violence** — directly experienced, witnessed, learned occurred to close other (violent/accidental), or **repeated/extreme exposure to aversive details** (first responders). Symptoms: **reexperiencing** (intrusive memories, nightmares, **flashbacks**), **avoidance**, **numbing/detachment**, **negative cognitions/mood**, **hyperarousal** (easily startled, irritable, quick to anger). First named in **DSM-III (1980)**; historical example **Samuel Pepys** after the 1666 Great Fire of London. Case: **the Joneses** (German shepherd attack; multiple family members developed PTSD; Jeff showed **survivor guilt**).
- **DSM-5 changes (vs DSM-IV):** added **"reckless or self-destructive behavior"** under arousal criterion E; added a **dissociative subtype** (depersonalization/derealization, *less* arousal than normal — responds differently to treatment).
- **Acute stress disorder:** PTSD-like reaction **within the first month** after trauma (introduced DSM-IV). ~50% of acute stress disorder cases progress to PTSD, but **52%** of eventual-PTSD cases did **not** meet acute stress disorder criteria — early reactions are poor predictors. **PTSD cannot be diagnosed until ≥1 month** post-trauma. **Delayed onset/expression**: threshold not met until **≥6 months** later.

> **DSM-5-TR criteria — PTSD (≥1 month duration):** A) exposure (experience/witness/learn-of-violent-or-accidental/repeated aversive-detail exposure); B) ≥1 **intrusion** symptom (intrusive memories, distressing dreams, dissociative flashbacks, distress/physiological reactivity to cues); C) persistent **avoidance** of trauma-related stimuli (memories/thoughts/feelings and/or external reminders); D) **negative alterations in cognitions/mood** (≥2: dissociative amnesia for trauma, exaggerated negative beliefs about self/others/world, distorted blame, persistent negative emotional state, diminished interest, detachment/estrangement, inability to feel positive emotions); E) **alterations in arousal/reactivity** (irritability/aggression, **reckless/self-destructive behavior**, hypervigilance, exaggerated startle, concentration problems, sleep disturbance); F) duration >1 month; G) clinically significant impairment. **Specify: with dissociative symptoms; with delayed expression.**

- **Statistics:** lifetime **6.8%**, past-year **3.5%** (adolescents 3.9%). **Conditional risk depends on trauma type** (Breslau): highest for **rape (49%)**, **held captive/tortured/kidnapped (53.8%)**, badly beaten (31.9%); lower for natural disaster (3.8%), car crash (6.1%). **Proximity matters**: 9/11 — 7.5% PTSD/ASD south of 110th St but **20%** south of Canal St; ~10.5% (75,000) NYC schoolchildren grades 4-12. WWII London air-raid survivors and Beirut students showed **low** rates (less direct death exposure). Iraq/Afghanistan vets: **4.3%** overall, **7.6%** with combat exposure vs 1.4% without (better than feared 30%). Vietnam vets **18.7%**; POWs **67%**. Chronic course; independently predicts suicide attempts.
- **Causes:** the **one disorder with a known precipitating cause** (the trauma) — but development is bio-psycho-social. Intensity of exposure matters (but 33% of Vietnam POWs did *not* develop it — e.g., John McCain). **Genetic:** family history of anxiety = generalized bio vulnerability; MZ twin concordance > DZ (r .28-.41 vs .11-.24); **stress-diathesis** — short-allele (SS) **serotonin transporter gene** ↑ acute stress symptoms (NIU shooting study). **Generalized psych vulnerability** (early uncontrollable events; family instability ↑ risk). Higher intelligence ↓ exposure; externalizing children ↑ exposure. **Social support is protective** (parents/friends/teachers; Hurricane Andrew/Charley studies); positive coping protective, anger/blame harmful; lack of social support partly explains high Vietnam-vet rates. **Neurobiology:** elevated/restricted **CRF → HPA axis** overactivity; **hippocampal damage** (war PTSD, childhood sexual abuse survivors, firefighters) → memory deficits — possibly reversible (Cushing's disease hippocampal volume ↑ 10% after treatment). Model: **true alarm** → **learned alarm** to trauma reminders + anxious apprehension, moderated by social support/coping.
- **Treatment:** psychologically, victims should **face the trauma, process emotions, develop coping** (psychoanalytic term: **catharsis**). **Imaginal exposure** / develop and review a **trauma narrative**; **cognitive therapy** to correct guilt/self-blame. Marcie treated with graded exposure hierarchy (watched brother first; instant photos; drawings). **Early structured intervention** prevents PTSD (Ehlers: 11% developed PTSD after 12 sessions CT vs 61% self-help booklet vs 55% repeated assessment) and beats medication; **single forced debriefing can be harmful**. Couple-based treatment improves PTSD + relationship satisfaction. **Drugs:** SSRIs (Prozac, Paxil) help.

> ICD-11 note: ICD-11 streamlines PTSD to a **6-symptom core** (reexperiencing in the here-and-now, avoidance, sense of current threat/hypervigilance) and — unlike DSM-5 — adds a **separate diagnosis of Complex PTSD (CPTSD)**: PTSD core **plus** disturbances in self-organization (affect dysregulation, negative self-concept, relationship difficulties), typically after prolonged/repeated trauma. DSM-5/TR has **no** Complex PTSD category; it instead uses the dissociative subtype. ICD-11 also has Prolonged Grief Disorder (DSM-5-TR added one too).

### Adjustment Disorders
- Anxious/depressive reactions to **identifiable life stress** that are **milder** than acute stress disorder/PTSD but still impairing; stressor is **not traumatic**. Considered **"chronic"** if symptoms persist >6 months after the stressor/consequences are removed. Historically a residual category (little research).

### Attachment Disorders
- Disturbed, developmentally inappropriate behavior emerging **before age 5** from **inadequate/abusive caregiving** (e.g., multiple foster placements, neglect) → failure to meet basic emotional needs. DSM-5 split the old "reactive attachment disorder" into **two**:
  - **Reactive Attachment Disorder** — **inhibited/emotionally withdrawn** type; seldom seeks/responds to comfort; limited positive affect, fearfulness/sadness.
  - **Disinhibited Social Engagement Disorder** — **indiscriminately social/disinhibited**; approaches/accompanies unfamiliar adults without checking back with caregiver.

## OBSESSIVE-COMPULSIVE AND RELATED DISORDERS

> New DSM-5 class linking disorders by **driven repetitive behaviors** + similar course/treatment response. Brings together: **OCD** (moved out of anxiety disorders), **hoarding disorder** (new separate category), **BDD** (moved from somatoform disorders), **trichotillomania** (moved from impulse-control disorders), and **excoriation/skin-picking disorder** (new).

### Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD)
- The "devastating culmination of the anxiety disorders" — the disorder most likely to need hospitalization or, as a last resort, psychosurgery. Danger here is an **internal thought, image, or impulse** (not external object).
- **Obsessions** = intrusive, mostly nonsensical thoughts/images/urges the individual tries to resist/eliminate. **Compulsions** = thoughts or actions used to **suppress obsessions and provide relief**; can be **behavioral** (washing, checking) or **mental** (counting, praying, repeating words); often "**magical**" (no logical relation to the obsession). Believed to reduce stress/prevent a dreaded event. Case **Richard** ("Enslaved by Ritual"): abandoned hygiene, elaborate eating/walking rituals, intrusive sex/aggression/religion thoughts.

> **DSM-5-TR criteria — OCD:** A) presence of **obsessions, compulsions, or both** (obsessions: recurrent intrusive unwanted thoughts/urges/images causing distress that the person tries to suppress/neutralize; compulsions: repetitive behaviors or mental acts driven by an obsession or rigid rules, aimed at reducing distress/preventing a dreaded event, not realistically connected or clearly excessive); B) **time-consuming (>1 hour/day)** or clinically significant distress/impairment; C) not due to substance/medical condition; D) not better explained by another disorder. **Specify insight:** with good/fair insight; with poor insight; with **absent insight/delusional** beliefs. **Specify if tic-related.**
> DSM-5-TR note: DSM-5 added the insight specifiers (incl. delusional) and the tic-related specifier — a change from DSM-IV, which lacked the delusional/insight gradient.

- **Four types of obsessions** (and associated compulsions; Bloch et al., 2008):
  1. **Symmetry/exactness/"just right"** — **26.7%** (most common) → ordering, arranging, repeating.
  2. **Forbidden thoughts or actions** (aggressive/sexual/religious) — **21%** → checking, avoidance, reassurance-seeking.
  3. **Cleaning/contamination** — **15.9%** → washing, gloves/masks.
  4. **Hoarding** — **15.4%** (note: hoarding is now its own disorder).
  - **Thought-action fusion** = equating the thought with the action it represents. **Thought suppression backfires** (Wegner) → increases the thought's frequency.
- **Statistics:** lifetime **1.6-2.3%**, 1-yr **1%**. Sex ratio **~1:1** (boys develop earlier; equal by mid-adolescence). Median onset **19** (males 13-15, females 20-24); chronic. Cross-culturally similar; content shaped by culture (religious/cleanliness themes in Saudi Arabia/Egypt; contamination in India).
- **Tic disorder & PANDAS:** tic disorder commonly co-occurs (esp. children/families); complex tics with vocalizations = **Tourette's disorder**; 10-40% of child/adolescent OCD had tics; tic-related obsessions almost always about **symmetry**. **PANDAS** = Pediatric Autoimmune Neuropsychiatric Disorder Associated with Streptococcal infection (abrupt onset post-strep; more male; remits with antibiotics; clumsiness); broadened to **PANS** (Pediatric Acute-onset Neuropsychiatric Syndrome).
- **Causes:** intrusive thoughts are normal (13% of community sample have moderate obsessions/compulsions). Need generalized bio/psych vulnerability + **specific psychological vulnerability** that **some thoughts are dangerous/unacceptable** and one is responsible (→ thought-action fusion, often from childhood attitudes of **excessive responsibility/guilt**; linked to strength — not type — of religious belief). Suppression/neutralization → compulsions that fail.
- **Treatment:**
  - **Drugs:** serotonin reuptake inhibitors — **clomipramine** and SSRIs — benefit up to **60%**; high relapse off drug.
  - **Psychological (most effective):** **Exposure and Ritual Prevention (ERP)** — rituals actively prevented while patient gradually exposed to feared thoughts/situations → reality testing that no harm occurs. Foa et al. (2005): **ERP 86%** response vs **clomipramine 48%**; combining added no advantage; drug-only high relapse. Cognitive treatments (targeting overestimated threat, inflated responsibility, need for perfection/certainty) about as effective as ERP.
  - **Psychosurgery (last resort):** **cingulotomy** (lesion to cingulate bundle; ~30% benefit), **capsulotomy** (~35% benefit but serious side effects), **deep brain stimulation** (reversible advantage).

### Body Dysmorphic Disorder (BDD)
- Preoccupation with an **imagined/slight defect in appearance** ("imagined ugliness," formerly **dysmorphophobia**) in someone who looks reasonably normal. Case **Jim** ("Ashamed to Be Seen"): convinced his head was square. Moved from somatoform to OCD-related class (co-occurs with OCD, shared family history, same onset/course, similar abnormal brain functioning, same effective treatments).

> **DSM-5-TR criteria — BDD:** A) preoccupation with ≥1 defect/flaw in appearance **not observable or appearing slight to others**; B) at some point, **repetitive behaviors** (mirror checking, grooming, skin picking, reassurance seeking) or **mental acts** (comparing appearance) in response to concerns; C) clinically significant distress/impairment; D) not better explained by an eating disorder (if concern is weight/fat). **Specify insight** (good/fair, poor, absent/delusional). **Specify: with muscle dysmorphia** (preoccupied that build is too small/insufficiently muscular — nearly unique to men).
> DSM-5-TR note: DSM-5 added the **repetitive behaviors criterion (B)**, the insight specifiers, and the **muscle dysmorphia** specifier (changes from DSM-IV's somatoform-era criteria).

- **Features:** average **5-7** body areas of concern; most common = **skin (80%)**, **hair (58%)**, **nose (39%)**. Mirror checking or mirror avoidance; **excessive tanning**, grooming, **skin picking**; **ideas of reference**. Delusional vs nondelusional differ little (delusional = more severe, less educated, doesn't respond to antipsychotics) → DSM-5 gives a BDD diagnosis regardless of insight.
- **Statistics:** ~**1-2%** community, 2-13% student samples; **lifelong** course untreated (one patient 71 years). **Equal in men and women** (men: build/genitals/thinning hair, more severe, **muscle dysmorphia**; women: more varied areas, more often comorbid eating disorder). Onset early adolescence-20s, peak **16-17**. High suicidality (24-27.5% past attempts). QoL/impairment worse than depression/diabetes/recent MI on some measures.
- **Causes:** little known; no family/genetic/biological data. Psychoanalytic idea = **displacement**. Best etiological hint = comorbidity/similarity with **OCD** (similar etiology) and **social anxiety** (taijin kyofusho link; negative-evaluation concerns as important as self-evaluation).
- **Treatment — same two as OCD:** (1) **serotonin-blocking drugs** — clomipramine > desipramine; fluoxetine 53% vs 18% placebo (effective even for delusional type); (2) **Exposure and Response Prevention (CBT)** — 82% responded (Rosen). CBT > medication alone for durability. **Plastic surgery / dermatology is bad business** — 76.4% of BDD patients sought medical/surgical treatment; **81-88%** dissatisfied or worse after surgery; they return for more surgery or sue. Estimated **8-25%** of plastic-surgery requesters have BDD. Surgeons should screen them out. Some patients perform dangerous self-surgery (staple gun, filing teeth, cutting thighs).

### Hoarding Disorder
- New **separate** DSM-5 category. Three core features: **excessive acquisition**, **difficulty discarding anything**, and **living with excessive clutter / gross disorganization**. Prevalence **2-5%** (twice OCD), roughly equal men/women, worldwide. Begins in teens; great pleasure ("retail therapy"), then strong distress at discarding (potential use / sentimental value / extension of identity). Average treatment-seeking age **~50**; often live alone; little insight.
- Differs from OCD: OCD waxes/wanes, **hoarding worsens each decade**. Distinct neural deficits in identifying emotional significance of objects. **Animal hoarding** = special group (often middle-aged/elderly women, 30+ animals, fail to care for them, unsanitary; attribute human characteristics to animals; little insight). Hoarding fires = only a tiny fraction of residential fires but **24% of all fire-related fatalities**.
- **Treatment:** assign different values to objects, reduce discard-anxiety; preliminary results modest (less successful than OCD).

### Trichotillomania (Hair Pulling Disorder) and Excoriation (Skin Picking Disorder)
- **Trichotillomania** = recurrent **pulling out one's own hair** (scalp, eyebrows, arms) → noticeable hair loss, distress, social impairment; often concealed. **1-5%** of college students, more females. Possible genetic mutation in a small subset.
- **Excoriation (skin picking disorder)** = repetitive/compulsive **skin picking** → tissue damage; **1-5%** of population with noticeable damage; largely female. (Distinguish from BDD skin picking, which aims to "improve" appearance.)
- Both **moved from impulse-control disorders** into the OCD-related class (co-occur with OCD, BDD, and each other). **DSM-5-TR note:** DSM-5 **removed the DSM-IV "tension/relief" criteria** (rising tension before, gratification/relief after) because a substantial subset don't experience tension relief (Nock et al., 2011).
- **Treatment:** **Habit Reversal Training** (HRT) has the most evidence — increase awareness of the behavior just before it begins, then substitute a competing harmless behavior (chewing gum, applying lotion); results possible in ~4 sessions. SSRIs hold some promise (more for trichotillomania; mixed for excoriation).

## DSM Controversy: Categorical vs Dimensional Classification
- DSM-5 split the old DSM-IV anxiety class into **3 classes** and added **10 disorders** (by splitting, relocating, or introducing). Brown & Barlow (2009) propose a **dimensional system** for **"emotional disorders"** (anxiety + depression spectrum) rather than many categorical diagnoses.
- Illustrated by **Mr. S.** (car accident + Vietnam trauma → met criteria for PTSD + GAD + mild depression). Dimensional profile rates him across: **AN** (anxiety), **DEP** (depression), **SOM** (somatic anxiety), **PAS** (panic/autonomic surges), **IC** (intrusive cognitions), **SOC** (social evaluation), **TRM** (trauma), **AV-BI** (behavioral/interoceptive avoidance), **AV-CE** (cognitive/emotional avoidance). Captures severity of features present together that a single category misses — "not ready for DSM-5... might be ready for DSM-6."

> ICD-11 note: the dimensional impulse the chapter describes is realized most fully in ICD-11's **personality disorder** model (fully dimensional — severity + trait domains, replacing categorical clusters), and partially in DSM-5-TR's Section III **Alternative Model for Personality Disorders**. Anxiety/OCD/trauma disorders themselves remain **categorical** in both current systems.

## Quick exam anchors
- Anxiety = future-oriented + tension; Fear = present danger + sympathetic surge; Panic attack = fear at the wrong time (≥4 of 13 symptoms, peak within minutes).
- **Triple vulnerability** (generalized biological + generalized psychological + specific psychological) underlies *every* disorder in the chapter.
- Best psychological treatments by disorder: GAD → CBT (long-term > drugs); PD → PCT/interoceptive exposure; specific phobia → exposure (blood phobia → applied tension); SAD → cognitive therapy + social mishap exposure; PTSD → imaginal exposure / trauma narrative + cognitive therapy; OCD & BDD → **Exposure and Ritual/Response Prevention (ERP/ERP)**; hoarding → cognitive-behavioral discarding work (modest); trich/excoriation → **Habit Reversal Training**.
- DSM-5 relocations to remember: OCD ← anxiety; BDD ← somatoform; trich ← impulse-control; PTSD/acute stress ← anxiety; hoarding & excoriation = new.
