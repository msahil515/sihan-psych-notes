  1. Parts of the autonomic nervous system are: (Amity 2023)

    • aSympathetic and parasympathetic
    • bCentral and peripheral
    • cVoluntary and involuntary
    • dCerebrospinal and cortical
    ✓ Answer: (a) Sympathetic and parasympathetic
    Divisions of the autonomic nervous system
    The concept

    What it isThe autonomic nervous system (ANS) runs the body's involuntary functions. These are its two main divisions: the sympathetic and the parasympathetic.

    Key noteMany books add a third, the enteric system of the gut wall.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (b) Central and peripheralDivides the whole nervous system, not the ANS.
    • (c) Voluntary and involuntaryDescribes somatic versus autonomic control, not the parts of the ANS.
    • (d) Cerebrospinal and corticalNot a division at all.

  2. Which of the following is the correct path of a reflex arc? (Amity 2023)

    • aAfferent neuron to interneuron to afferent neuron
    • bAfferent neuron to afferent neuron to interneuron
    • cAfferent neuron to interneuron to efferent neuron
    • dEfferent neuron to interneuron to the brain
    ✓ Answer: (c) Afferent neuron to interneuron to efferent neuron
    Spinal reflex arc
    The concept

    How it worksThe signal enters the spinal cord by a sensory (afferent) neuron, crosses an interneuron in the grey matter and leaves by a motor (efferent) neuron to the effector.

    Exam tipAfferent arrives, efferent exits. The knee-jerk even skips the interneuron.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (a) Afferent neuron to interneuron to afferent neuronEnds in an afferent neuron; an arc must end in an efferent neuron to reach the effector.
    • (b) Afferent neuron to afferent neuron to interneuronTwo afferent neurons and no way out; the arc must end in an efferent neuron.
    • (d) Efferent neuron to interneuron to the brainRuns to the brain, but the arc closes in the spinal cord, not the brain.

  3. White matter is primarily composed of (JEMAS 2025)

    • aMyelinated axons.
    • bMyelinated dendrites.
    • cCell bodies.
    • dUnmyelinated axons.
    ✓ Answer: (a) Myelinated axons
    Myelin and white matter
    The concept

    Why whiteThe fat of the myelin sheath around axons makes the tissue look white.

    The contrastGrey matter is made of cell bodies and unmyelinated axons.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (b) Myelinated dendrites.Dendrites are not myelinated, so they cannot make white matter.
    • (c) Cell bodies.Cell bodies make up grey matter, not white.
    • (d) Unmyelinated axons.Unmyelinated axons belong to grey matter; without myelin there is no white.

  7. Which of the following is an inhibitory neurotransmitter? (JEMAS 2025)

    ✓ Answer: (b) GABA
    GABA, main inhibitory transmitter

    How it worksAt GABA-A receptors it opens Cl- channels and produces IPSPs (inhibitory postsynaptic potentials).

    Why the other options are wrong

  10. Valium, Xanax, Rohypnol, and Halcion are: (Amity 2023)

    • aBenzodiazepines
    • bBarbiturates
    • cSedatives
    • dHypnotics
    ✓ Answer: (a) Benzodiazepines
    Benzodiazepines, one chemical class
    The concept

    The drugsValium is diazepam, Xanax alprazolam, Rohypnol flunitrazepam and Halcion triazolam: all one chemical class, the benzodiazepines.

    How they workThey raise the frequency of GABA-A chloride channel opening.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (b) BarbituratesA different class: they raise the duration of opening, not its frequency.
    • (c) SedativesA broad functional label that also covers alcohol and barbiturates, not a chemical class.
    • (d) HypnoticsA broad functional label that also covers alcohol and barbiturates, not a chemical class.

  12. A 45-year-old man with schizophrenia has been on antipsychotic medication for years. He presents with repetitive, involuntary, and purposeless movements of his mouth, tongue, and face, including lip-smacking and grimacing. This condition is most likely: (Real PYQ 2024)

    • aAkathisia
    • bAcute Dystonia
    • cTardive Dyskinesia
    • dParkinsonism
    ✓ Answer: (c) Tardive Dyskinesia
    Tardive dyskinesia, late extrapyramidal effect
    The concept

    What it isInvoluntary orofacial movements after months or years of neuroleptic treatment are tardive dyskinesia (tardive means late).

    Key noteIt is the late, often irreversible extrapyramidal effect.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (a) AkathisiaInner restlessness and pacing, which comes within days to weeks.
    • (b) Acute DystoniaMuscle spasms that come within hours to days, not years.

  13. Which neuroscientific technique provides the highest temporal resolution for studying neural activity? (RML 2025)

    • dCT scan
    ✓ Answer: (c) EEG

    The concept

  16. The Mach bands phenomenon is best explained by : (IHBAS 2025)

    • aRod-cone dichotomy in the fovea
    • bOpponent-process theory of color vision
    • cLateral inhibition in the retina
    • dTemporal summation in photoreceptors
    ✓ Answer: (c) Lateral inhibition in the retina
    Lateral inhibition, Mach bands
    The concept

    Key termLateral inhibition means neighbouring retinal cells suppress each other.

    How it worksAt a light-dark border, cells on the bright side receive less inhibition from their dark neighbours and cells on the dark side receive more.

    The resultThe edge is exaggerated into a brighter and a darker strip: the Mach bands.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (a) Rod-cone dichotomy in the foveaConcerns dim-light versus colour vision, not edges.
    • (b) Opponent-process theory of color visionExplains colour coding and afterimages, not brightness at an edge.
    • (d) Temporal summation in photoreceptorsThe adding of weak signals over time, not contrast at an edge.

  17. Difficulty in seeing objects at a distance is called : (IHBAS 2024)

    • dVisual Field Defects
    ✓ Answer: (a) Myopia
    Myopia (near-sightedness)
    The concept

    What it isIn myopia (near-sightedness) the eyeball is too long, so distant objects focus in front of the retina and blur.

    The fixA concave lens corrects it.

    Why the other options are wrong

  18. Which of the following areas of the brain is involved in controlling movements of the body? (Amity 2023)

    • aPrimary
    • bSecondary
    • cSupplementary
    • dAll of the above
    ✓ Answer: (d) All of the above
    Motor cortex hierarchy
    The concept

    The executorMovement is controlled by a hierarchy. Primary motor cortex (area 4, precentral gyrus) is the main cortical output.

    The plannersSecondary motor cortex, the premotor cortex and supplementary motor area, programmes the patterns of movement that primary cortex executes.

    Why allNo single area does the job alone.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (a) PrimaryThe main output, but it executes patterns the secondary areas programme.
    • (b) SecondaryProgrammes movement patterns but needs primary cortex to execute them.
    • (c) SupplementaryPart of secondary motor cortex; it programmes movement but does not act alone.

  20. Which cluster of symptoms is seen in Kluver-Bucy syndrome? (JEMAS 2025)

    • aAgnosia, aphasia, hyper-orality, hyperphagia, hypersexuality
    • bAgnosia, avolition, alogia, asexuality

    ✓ Answer: (a) Agnosia, aphasia, hyper-orality, hyperphagia, hypersexuality
    Kluver-Bucy syndrome, anterior temporal lobe and amygdala

    CauseBilateral anterior temporal lobe and amygdala damage.

    SymptomsPsychic blindness (agnosia), hyperorality, hyperphagia and hypersexuality, with aphasia in human cases.

  21. What is the term for the brain's process of regulating its own responses to stress by controlling hormone release? (IHBAS 2024)

    • aNeuroplasticity
    • bHomeostasis
    • cAllostasis
    • dCircadian rhythm
    ✓ Answer: (c) Allostasis
    Sterling and Eyer, allostasis (1988)
    The concept

    What it isAllostasis is stability through change: the brain anticipates demands and adjusts hormone release.

    Who named itSterling and Eyer defined it in 1988.

    Why the other options are wrong

  22. A lesion of the ventromedial hypothalamus (VMH) produces: (Recall set)

    • aLoss of circadian rhythms
    • bAphagia and adipsia
    • cDiabetes insipidus
    • dHyperphagia and obesity
    ✓ Answer: (d) Hyperphagia and obesity
    Hetherington and Ranson, VMH satiety centre (1940)
    The concept

    What it isThe VMH was the classic satiety centre (Hetherington and Ranson, 1940).

    Why this oneDestroy the satiety centre and the animal keeps eating: hyperphagia and obesity.

    Why the other options are wrong

  24. Masters and Johnson's sexual response cycle is distinct from Kaplan's model. Which of the following correctly reflects Masters and Johnson's sequence? (IHBAS 2025)

  25. A person suffered a head injury in a road accident several years ago. Since the accident, he is unable to form new memories.   26. According to Karl Lashley's research on memory storage, where did he conclude that memories are primarily stored in the brain? (Real PYQ 2025)

    • aIn specific localized brain regions dedicated to particular memories
    • bDistributed throughout the entire brain cortex
    • cExclusively in the hippocampus
    • dOnly in the frontal and temporal lobes
    ✓ Answer: (b) Distributed throughout the entire brain cortex
    Karl Lashley, mass action and equipotentiality
    The concept

    The findingNo cortical lesion erased a maze habit in Lashley's rats; he found no localised engram.

    Two principlesThe deficit depended on how much cortex was removed (mass action), not where (equipotentiality).

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (a) In specific localized brain regions dedicated to particular memoriesThe opposite of his result: he found no localised engram.
    • (c) Exclusively in the hippocampusThe hippocampus's role in forming memories emerged only later, with H.M.
    • (d) Only in the frontal and temporal lobesThe deficit depended on how much cortex was removed, not which lobes.

  27. Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome results in: (Amity 2023)

    • dNone
    ✓ Answer: (c) Both a and b

    • (b) Loss of muscle coordinationTrue (ataxia), but only half the picture.
    • (d) NoneFails because a and b are both features of the syndrome.

Extrapyramidal side effects come from D2 blockade in the nigrostriatal pathway and appear in order: acute dystonia (hours to days), akathisia (days to weeks, inner restlessness), parkinsonism (weeks) and tardive dyskinesia (months to years, orofacial movements, may be irreversible); akathisia is a secondary movement disorder. asked 10 times
