  1. Biologically based characteristic way of reacting is known as (JEMAS 2025)

    • aDisposition
    • bCharacter
    • cTrait
    • dTemperament
    ✓ Answer: (d) Temperament
    Definition of temperament
    The concept

    What it isTemperament is the biologically based, early-appearing emotional style: a person's characteristic way of reacting.

    Why this oneThe stem stresses a biological basis, and that is exactly what sets temperament apart from the other terms.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (a) DispositionToo loose a term: any tendency to act a certain way, with no claim about biological origin.
    • (b) CharacterPersonality evaluated morally, and it is learned, not biologically based.
    • (c) TraitA descriptive dimension of personality, not defined by its biological origin.

  2. Sheldon's theory that people with different body types have different personalities has been: (RML 2020)

    • asupported by research
    • bdiscredited
    • cshown to be accurate for thin people but not overweight people
    • dshown to be accurate for women but not for men
    ✓ Answer: (b) discredited
    Sheldon, body type (somatotype) theory
    The concept

    The theorySheldon claimed that body type predicts temperament.

    Why it failedHe rated physique and temperament himself, so his expectations inflated the correlations. Independent studies found only weak links.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (a) supported by researchThe opposite: independent studies found only weak links once Sheldon's own biased ratings were removed.
    • (c) shown to be accurate for thin people but not overweight peopleInvented subgroup: the theory holds for no subgroup, thin or overweight.
    • (d) shown to be accurate for women but not for menCannot be right: his subjects were men, and the theory holds for no subgroup.

  3. Physiognomy, an early approach to personality study, was based on : (IHBAS 2025)

    • bDreams and unconscious motives
    • cHandwriting style analysis
    • dFacial features as indicators of character
    ✓ Answer: (d) Facial features as indicators of character
    Physiognomy: reading character from the face
    The concept

    What it isPhysiognomy treated facial features as outward signs of inner character.

    Exam tipMatch each early method to its clue: face is physiognomy, skull bumps phrenology, handwriting graphology.

  7. A woman harassed by her boss at work, initiates an argument with her husband. She is using which of the following ego's defense mechanisms: (RML 2024)

    • aProjection
    • bIdentification
    • cSublimation
    • dDisplacement
    ✓ Answer: (d) Displacement
    Freudian defence mechanism: displacement
    The concept

    What it isDisplacement redirects a feeling from a threatening target onto a safer one.

    Why this oneHer anger at the boss, who could punish her, is redirected onto a safer target, her husband.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (a) ProjectionShe would attribute her own anger to someone else, not redirect it.
    • (b) IdentificationShe would take on another's qualities, not redirect anger.
    • (c) SublimationShe would channel the anger into a socially valued activity, not into a fight.

  8. Which type of defence mechanism is dissociation? (NIMHANS 2026 recall)

    • aMature
    • bImmature
    • cNeurotic
    • dConscious
    ✓ Answer: (c) Neurotic
    Vaillant's hierarchy of defences (1977)
    The concept

    The hierarchyVaillant (1977) ranked defences in levels from immature to mature. Level III is the neurotic level.

    Neurotic defencesLevel III groups dissociation with intellectualisation, isolation, repression, displacement and reaction formation.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (a) MatureThe top level (Level IV): sublimation, humour, altruism, suppression; dissociation is not among them.
    • (b) ImmatureThis level holds projection, acting out and the like, not dissociation.
    • (d) ConsciousDefences are not conscious as a class; only suppression is deliberate.

  9. Which of the following defense mechanisms is not involved in obsessive-compulsive disorder? (IHBAS 2020)

    • bRepression
    • cIsolation
    • dUndoing
    ✓ Answer: (b) Repression
    Freud, defences in obsessional neurosis
    The concept

    In OCDIn obsessional neurosis the disturbing idea stays conscious; the defences work on its feeling and meaning instead.

    Hysteria contrastRepression, which pushes the idea itself out of awareness, is the hallmark of hysteria.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (a) Reaction formationInvolved: it turns the disturbing idea into its opposite.
    • (c) IsolationInvolved: the idea stays conscious but its feeling is isolated from it.
    • (d) UndoingInvolved: rituals undo the idea, as if cancelling it.

  12. According to Adler, middle children tend to be: (RML 2022)

    • dFilled with feelings of inferiority
    ✓ Answer: (b) Competitive

  18. According to Raymond Cattell's classification of personality data sources, which of the following is NOT recognized as a legitimate data type? (Real PYQ 2025)

    • aQ-data (Questionnaire data)
    • bL-data (Life record data)
    • cT-data (Objective test data)
    • dP-data (Projective data)
    ✓ Answer: (d) P-data
    Raymond Cattell, L-, Q- and T-data
    The concept

    Three sourcesCattell used exactly three: L-data (life records), Q-data (questionnaires) and T-data (objective tests).

    The trapHe classed hard-to-fake tasks such as inkblots as T-data, so there is no projective category. His P-technique is a research design, not a data type.

    Why the other options are not the answer
    • (a) Q-data (Questionnaire data)Legitimate: self-report from questionnaires, one of Cattell's three sources.
    • (b) L-data (Life record data)Legitimate: life records, one of the three sources.
    • (c) T-data (Objective test data)Legitimate: objective, hard-to-fake tests; this is where Cattell put inkblots.
