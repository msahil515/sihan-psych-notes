  1. To describe a predisposition towards and risk triggers for a mental disorder, modern theorists use a (JEMAS 2025)

    • aDiathesis-Stress model
    • bNeurodevelopmental model
    • cClinical-Reductionist model
    • dSyndrome-Sign model
    ✓ Answer: (a) Diathesis-stress model
    Diathesis-stress model of psychopathology
    The concept

    What it isThe diathesis is the predisposition and stress is the trigger.

    How it worksDisorder appears when stress exceeds the person's threshold: high vulnerability needs little stress, low vulnerability needs a lot.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (b) Neurodevelopmental modelTraces schizophrenia to faulty early brain development; it explains origin, not predisposition plus trigger.
    • (c) Clinical-Reductionist modelNot a real model; a made-up distractor.
    • (d) Syndrome-Sign modelAlso not a real model; a filler distractor.

  2. All of the following statements are TRUE about exceptions to the rule of confidentiality EXCEPT: (RML 2020)

    • aa court order requires the release of information
    • bthe information is essential to safeguard the health of others (duty to protect; warn)
    • cthe information is probative of guilt or innocence in a criminal matter
    • dthere are never exceptions to the rule of confidentiality
    ✓ Answer: (d) There are never exceptions to the rule of confidentiality
    Limits of confidentiality; Tarasoff duty to warn
    The concept

    Key ideaConfidentiality is not absolute: law and ethics recognise set exceptions.

    The exceptionsA court order, the Tarasoff duty to warn and protect, and evidence a court needs in a criminal case are all recognised exceptions, so (a) to (c) are true.

    Why this oneOption (d) claims there are never exceptions, which is false, so it is the EXCEPT answer.

    Why the other options are not the answer
    • (a) a court order requires the release of informationTrue: a court order is a recognised legal exception to confidentiality.
    • (b) the information is essential to safeguard the health of others (duty to protect; warn)True: the Tarasoff duty to warn and protect allows disclosure to safeguard others.
    • (c) the information is probative of guilt or innocence in a criminal matterTrue: evidence a court needs in a criminal case is a recognised exception.

  3. Which of the following is a key legal provision under the Mental Healthcare Act, 2017? (IHBAS 2025)

    • aSuicide attempts are decriminalized, recognizing mental illness as a contributing factor
    • bElectroconvulsive therapy (ECT) can be administered without consent in all cases
    • cOnly psychiatrists can provide therapy; psychologists are excluded
    • dMental health establishments are exempt from registration if government-run
    ✓ Answer: (a) Suicide attempts are decriminalized, recognizing mental illness as a contributing factor
    Mental Healthcare Act 2017, Section 115
    The concept

    The provisionSection 115 presumes severe stress in anyone who attempts suicide, so the person is not punished.

    Why it mattersAttempted suicide is treated as a sign of distress needing care, not as a crime.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (b) Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) can be administered without consent in all casesFalse: treatment under the Act needs informed consent, and ECT is banned outright without anaesthesia and muscle relaxant.
    • (c) Only psychiatrists can provide therapy; psychologists are excludedFalse: clinical psychologists are mental health professionals under the Act.
    • (d) Mental health establishments are exempt from registration if government-runFalse: every establishment, government or private, must register; it was the 1987 Act that exempted government hospitals from licensing.

  6. In the context of psychopathology, a "formulation" differs from a "diagnosis" in that a formulation primarily aims to: (Real PYQ 2024)

    • bProvide a hypothesis about the cause, precipitants, and maintaining factors of an individual's difficulties
    • cDetermine the prognosis and likely course of the disorder over time
    • dRule out organic causes for the presenting psychological symptoms
    ✓ Answer: (b) A hypothesis about cause, precipitants and maintaining factors
    Case formulation, for example the 5 Ps
    The concept

    What it isA formulation is an individualised explanation of why this person has these difficulties now.

    How it is builtFrameworks like the 5 Ps (presenting, predisposing, precipitating, perpetuating and protective factors) organise the hypothesis.

    Versus diagnosisA diagnosis classifies the disorder; a formulation explains this person's causes and maintaining factors.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (a) Classify the client's symptoms according to a standardized nosological system like DSM-5Classifying symptoms against a nosological system is diagnosis, the very thing formulation is contrasted with.
    • (c) Determine the prognosis and likely course of the disorder over timePredicting course is prognosis, not formulation.
    • (d) Rule out organic causes for the presenting psychological symptomsRuling out organic causes belongs to differential diagnosis.

  7. 'Counter transference' in psychotherapy refers to (JEMAS 2025)

    • bclient's projection onto the therapist
    • cclient's resistance to therapy
    • dbehavioral reinforcement techniques
    ✓ Answer: (a) The therapist's emotional reactions to the client, based on the therapist's own unresolved conflict
    Freud's psychoanalytic concept of countertransference
    The concept

    What it isCountertransference runs from therapist to client: the therapist's own unresolved conflicts colour their feelings about the client.

    Direction is the keyTransference goes from client to therapist; countertransference is the reverse.

    Why the other options are wrong

    • (d) behavioral reinforcement techniquesReinforcement belongs to behaviour therapy, not to psychoanalytic concepts.

  10. A technique in which a response that inhibits anxiety is paired with an anxiety-provoking stimulus, step by step, to break the conditioned link between that stimulus and anxiety is: (NIMHANS 2026 recall)

    • aSystematic desensitization
    • bFlooding
    • cAversion therapy
    • dExposure and response prevention
    ✓ Answer: (a) Systematic desensitization
    Wolpe, systematic desensitisation (reciprocal inhibition)
    The concept

    How it worksWolpe paired relaxation, a response incompatible with anxiety, with each step of a graded hierarchy.

    The principleRelaxation blocks anxiety at each step, so the stimulus loses its link to anxiety: reciprocal inhibition.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (b) FloodingFull-intensity exposure with no hierarchy or relaxation; the trap is that both are exposure-based.
    • (c) Aversion therapyPairs a behaviour with an unpleasant stimulus to suppress it, not an anxiety-inhibiting response.
    • (d) Exposure and response preventionERP blocks rituals so that anxiety habituates; no competing response is paired.

  11. Exposure & response prevention procedure is based on behaviour therapy principal of : (IHBAS 2024)

    • aCounter conditioning
    • bHabituation and extinction
    • cReciprocal inhibition
    • dGraded exposure
    ✓ Answer: (b) Habituation and extinction
    ERP: habituation and extinction
    The concept

    HabituationKept in contact with the obsessional trigger and prevented from performing the ritual, the patient's anxiety falls by itself: habituation.

    ExtinctionThe feared outcome fails to occur, so the fear response weakens: extinction.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (a) Counter conditioningCounterconditioning underlies systematic desensitisation, which pairs a new response with the trigger.
    • (c) Reciprocal inhibitionAlso underlies systematic desensitisation, where relaxation inhibits anxiety; ERP pairs no competing response.
    • (d) Graded exposureDescribes how the steps are arranged, not why fear falls.

  12. The behavioral technique that involves removing a desirable stimulus following an undesirable behavior in order to reduce the frequency of that behavior is called: (Real PYQ 2025)

    • aNegative reinforcement
    • bResponse cost
    • cPositive punishment
    • dExtinction
    ✓ Answer: (b) Response cost
    Operant conditioning: response cost (negative punishment)
    The concept

    What it isResponse cost means taking away a reinforcer the person already has (tokens, privileges) after a behaviour.

    Where it fitsIt is a form of negative punishment: something desirable is removed, so the behaviour decreases.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (a) Negative reinforcementRemoves something unpleasant and increases behaviour; the trap is the word negative.
    • (c) Positive punishmentAdds something unpleasant after the behaviour, rather than removing something desirable.
    • (d) ExtinctionMerely withholds the reinforcer that maintained the behaviour; nothing the person already has is taken away.
