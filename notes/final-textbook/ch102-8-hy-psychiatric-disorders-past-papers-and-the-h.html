  1. Which of the following culture-bound syndromes is NOT reported in India? (Amity 2023)

    • aDhat Syndrome
    • bKoro
    • cSuchi-bai
    • dLatah
    ✓ Answer: (d) Latah
    Latah, a Malaysian and Indonesian culture-bound syndrome
    The concept

    What Latah isLatah is an exaggerated startle with echolalia, echopraxia, automatic obedience and blurted obscenities.

    Why this oneIt belongs to Malaysia and Indonesia, not India, so it is the syndrome not reported here.

    Why the other options are not the answer
    • (a) Dhat SyndromeReported in India: anxiety over semen loss.
    • (b) KoroReported in India: fear that the genitals are retracting, with epidemics in north-east India.
    • (c) Suchi-baiReported in India: obsessional cleanliness in Bengal.

  2. The diathesis-stress model of psychopathology suggests that mental disorders result from: (RML 2023)

    • aGenetic factors only
    • bEnvironmental factors only
    • cA combination of genetic predisposition and environmental stressors
    • dNeurobiological abnormalities only
    ✓ Answer: (c) A combination of genetic predisposition and environmental stressors
    Zubin and Spring, vulnerability-stress model (1977)
    The concept

    What it isThe diathesis is a genetic, biological or psychological vulnerability. It produces disorder only when a stressor sets it off.

    Who named itZubin and Spring (1977) called this the vulnerability-stress model.

    Exam tipEvery option ending in 'only' names a single cause, the one-dimensional thinking the model replaced.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (a) Genetic factors onlyNames a single cause; genes can be the diathesis, but without a stressor the model predicts no disorder.
    • (b) Environmental factors onlyNames a single cause; a stressor needs an existing vulnerability to set off.
    • (d) Neurobiological abnormalities onlyNames a single cause; a biological abnormality is one kind of diathesis, which still needs a stressor.

  3. A high false positive rate in diagnosis means: (IHBAS 2025)

    • aDiagnosis is too strict
    • bNormal people are diagnosed as disordered
    • cDiagnosis has low reliability
    • dDisordered people go undetected
    ✓ Answer: (b) Normal people are diagnosed as disordered
    Definition of a false positive
    The concept

    What it isA false positive wrongly labels a healthy person as ill.

    Why it happensIt happens when diagnostic criteria are too loose, so normal people get swept in.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (a) Diagnosis is too strictOver-strict criteria cause the opposite error, false negatives, where real cases are missed.
    • (c) Diagnosis has low reliabilityLow reliability means clinicians disagree with each other, a separate problem.
    • (d) Disordered people go undetectedThese are false negatives, the result of over-strict criteria.

  4. Hypnagogic hallucinations most commonly occur in: (RML 2024)

    • aSchizophrenia
    • bDelirium
    • cDrug dependence
    • dNormal
    ✓ Answer: (d) Normal
    Hypnagogic and hypnopompic hallucinations as normal experience
    The concept

    What they areHypnagogic hallucinations occur while falling asleep; hypnopompic ones occur on waking.

    Who gets themThey lie within normal experience and are commonest in healthy people. Narcolepsy also features them.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (a) SchizophreniaThe hallucinations of schizophrenia are mainly auditory and occur in clear consciousness.
    • (b) DeliriumDelirium brings visual and tactile hallucinations; hypnagogic ones are still commonest in healthy people.
    • (c) Drug dependenceDrugs bring visual and tactile hallucinations; hypnagogic ones are still commonest in healthy people.

  5. Our current mood and feelings are best described by the term: (RML 2024)

    • aEmotion
    • bMood
    • cAffect
    • dFeeling
    ✓ Answer: (c) Affect
    Definition of affect versus mood
    The concept

    What affect isAffect is the immediate, observed, moment-to-moment expression of emotion: the weather.

    Versus moodMood is the sustained state the person reports: the climate.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (a) EmotionThe broader term for a brief reaction, not the observed moment-to-moment expression.
    • (b) MoodThe sustained state the person reports (the climate), not the current moment.
    • (d) FeelingThe experienced side of an emotion, not its observed expression.

  6. Choose the correct arrangement of these stages of elevated mood based upon severity: i) Elation, ii) Exaltation, iii) Euphoria, iv) Ecstasy. (Amity 2023)

    • ai, ii, iii & iv
    • biii, i, ii & iv
    • civ, iii, ii & i
    • dNone of the above
    ✓ Answer: (b) iii, i, ii & iv
    Grades of elevated mood, euphoria to ecstasy
    The concept

    Lower gradesElevated mood rises from euphoria (mild; hypomania) through elation (moderate; mania).

    Upper gradesNext comes exaltation (intense, with grandiosity; severe mania), then ecstasy (rapture; stuporous or delirious mania).

    Why this oneSo the order is iii (euphoria), i (elation), ii (exaltation), iv (ecstasy).

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (a) i, ii, iii & ivPuts elation first, but euphoria is the mildest grade.
    • (c) iv, iii, ii & iReverses the scale, putting ecstasy, the most severe grade, first.
    • (d) None of the aboveFails because option (b) gives the correct order.

  8. In which of the following situations would a diagnosis of "global developmental delay" be inappropriate? (RML 2022)

    • aThe patient is a child who is too young to fully manifest specific symptoms or to complete requisite assessments.
    • bThe patient, a 7-year-old boy, has a full-scale IQ of 65 and severe impairment in adaptive functioning.

    ✓ Answer: (b) The 7-year-old with full-scale IQ 65 and severe adaptive impairment
    Global developmental delay, provisional label under age 5
    The concept

    What it isGlobal developmental delay is a provisional label for children under 5 whose severity cannot yet be reliably assessed.

    Why this oneA testable 7-year-old who meets both criteria (IQ 65, severe adaptive impairment) has intellectual disability, so the provisional label does not fit.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (a) The patient is a child who is too young to fully manifest specific symptoms or to complete requisite assessments.Appropriate: a child too young to be assessed is exactly the provisional case the label exists for.
    • (c) The patient's scores on psychometric tests suggest intellectual disability, but there is insufficient information about the patient's adaptive functional skills.Appropriate: without adaptive data, severity cannot yet be reliably assessed.
    • (d) The patient's impaired adaptive functioning suggests intellectual developmental disorder, but there is insufficient information about the level of cognitive impairment measured by standardized instruments.Appropriate: without standardised cognitive testing, severity cannot yet be reliably assessed.

    What EE isExpressed emotion (EE) means critical, hostile or over-involved attitudes in a patient's relatives.

    The evidenceBrown, Birley and Wing (1972) and Vaughn and Leff (1976) showed that such relatives predict relapse after discharge.

    Why the other options are wrong

    • (c) Negative symptoms of schizophreniaEE does not cause the illness or its negative symptoms; it predicts relapse.
    • (d) Violent behavior in schizophrenic patientsEE does not cause violence; its established link is with relapse.

    ✓ Answer: (b) Undoing

    The concept

    Why undoingUndoing, an act that symbolically cancels a forbidden thought, is the most characteristic, so it is the key.

  18. A client with OCD washes their hands excessively to neutralize the anxiety caused by intrusive thoughts of contamination. This hand-washing behavior is BEST conceptualized as a(n): (Real PYQ 2024)

    • aConditioned stimulus
    • bUnconditioned response
    • cAvoidance response / Safety behavior
    • dOperant behavior maintained by positive reinforcement
    ✓ Answer: (c) Avoidance response / Safety behavior
    Mowrer, two-factor theory
    The concept

    Two-factor theoryIn Mowrer's two-factor account, fear is learned by classical conditioning and kept going by avoidance.

    Why washing persistsThe washing removes anxiety, so it is negatively reinforced: an avoidance response or safety behaviour.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (a) Conditioned stimulusThe contamination thought is the conditioned stimulus; the washing is the response to it.
    • (b) Unconditioned responseAn unconditioned response is an unlearned reflex; hand-washing is learned.
    • (d) Operant behavior maintained by positive reinforcementNothing pleasant is added; anxiety is removed, so it is negative, not positive, reinforcement.

  21. The difference between malingering and factitious disorder lies in: (IHBAS 2025)

    • aThe presence or absence of external rewards
    • bWhether the individual believes the symptoms are real
    • cThe severity of symptoms
    • dAge of onset
    ✓ Answer: (a) The presence or absence of external rewards
    Malingering versus factitious disorder: external incentive
    The concept

    What they shareIn both malingering and factitious disorder people deliberately falsify symptoms and know they are false.

    The differenceThe malingerer seeks an external incentive such as money or escape from duty. Factitious disorder persists without obvious external reward, to occupy the sick role.

    Why the other options are wrong
    • (b) Whether the individual believes the symptoms are realBoth know their symptoms are false, so belief does not separate them.
    • (c) The severity of symptomsNot the distinguishing feature; the difference is whether an external reward is sought.
    • (d) Age of onsetNot the distinguishing feature; what separates them is motive, external reward or the sick role.
