# Ch 9: Psychopathology I, Psychotic, Mood, Anxiety, OCD & Trauma (with duration gating)

This chapter covers the four diagnostic territories that the RCI / NIMHANS-pattern entrance hits hardest: the schizophrenia spectrum, mood disorders (bipolar and depressive), anxiety disorders, the OCD-related spectrum, and trauma- and stressor-related disorders. The single most tested device across all of them is **duration gating**: the exact time thresholds that separate one diagnosis from another. Memorise the numbers, because the examiner builds distractors out of them.

## 1. The Schizophrenia Spectrum and Other Psychotic Disorders

Psychosis means a loss of contact with reality, manifesting through **delusions** (fixed false beliefs not amenable to evidence), **hallucinations** (perceptions without external stimulus, most commonly auditory in schizophrenia), **disorganised thinking** (inferred from disorganised speech, e.g. derailment, tangentiality, word salad), **grossly disorganised or abnormal motor behaviour** (including catatonia), and **negative symptoms**. DSM-5 conceptualises these conditions on a gradient of severity and duration.

### Positive versus negative symptoms

**Positive symptoms** are additions to normal experience: delusions, hallucinations, disorganised speech, and disorganised behaviour. They reflect, in the classic dopamine hypothesis, **mesolimbic dopaminergic hyperactivity**, and they respond relatively well to dopamine-blocking antipsychotics.

**Negative symptoms** are subtractions from normal functioning and are linked to **mesocortical dopaminergic hypoactivity**; they predict poorer prognosis and respond poorly to typical antipsychotics. The classic five, easily remembered as the "five A's," are: **avolition** (loss of motivated goal-directed behaviour), **alogia** (poverty of speech), **anhedonia** (inability to experience pleasure), **asociality** (lack of interest in social interaction), and **affective flattening / blunted affect** (diminished emotional expression). DSM-5 emphasises two core domains: **diminished emotional expression** and **avolition**.

### Schneiderian first-rank symptoms

Kurt **Schneider** (1959) proposed symptoms he believed strongly suggestive of schizophrenia (though not pathognomonic, and DSM-5 removed their special diagnostic weight). The **first-rank symptoms (FRS)** are exam favourites:
- **Audible thoughts** (thought echo / écho de la pensée).
- **Voices arguing or discussing** the patient in the third person.
- **Voices commenting** on one's actions (running commentary).
- **Thought withdrawal, insertion, and broadcasting** (passivity of thought).
- **Made feelings, made impulses, made volitional acts** (passivity of affect, impulse, and volition; "made" = imposed by an external force).
- **Somatic passivity** (bodily sensations imposed by an outside agency).
- **Delusional perception** (a normal perception given a delusional meaning).

### The duration-gated psychotic disorders

This is the heart of the chapter's psychotic section. The same symptom picture is named differently depending on how long it has lasted:

| Disorder | Total duration | Active-phase note | Functional decline required? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Brief psychotic disorder** | **>1 day to <1 month**, full return to premorbid level | Often stress-triggered (may specify "with marked stressor") | No formal criterion |
| **Schizophreniform disorder** | **1 to 6 months** | Same symptom criteria as schizophrenia | NOT required |
| **Schizophrenia** | **>=6 months** total | **>=1 month** of active-phase symptoms (less if treated) | YES, marked decline in functioning |

**Schizophrenia (Criterion A)** requires two or more of the following for a significant portion of one month, and at least one must be from items 1 to 3: (1) delusions, (2) hallucinations, (3) disorganised speech, (4) grossly disorganised or catatonic behaviour, (5) negative symptoms. The 6-month total may include prodromal and residual phases (during which only attenuated or negative symptoms appear). Marked social or occupational dysfunction is mandatory, distinguishing it from schizophreniform disorder where decline is not required.

A memory hook for gating: **<1 month = brief; 1 to 6 months = schizophreniform; >=6 months = schizophrenia.**

### Catatonia

**Catatonia** is a psychomotor syndrome, not exclusive to schizophrenia (it occurs in mood disorders and medical conditions too). DSM-5 requires **>=3 of 12** signs: stupor, catalepsy (passive induction of a posture held against gravity), **waxy flexibility** (slight, even resistance to repositioning), mutism, negativism, posturing, mannerism, stereotypy, agitation, grimacing, **echolalia** (mimicking speech), and **echopraxia** (mimicking movements). Severe catatonia is a medical emergency and classically responds to **benzodiazepines** (lorazepam) and **ECT**.

### Schizoaffective disorder and delusional disorder

**Schizoaffective disorder** sits between schizophrenia and mood disorders. The key rule: a major mood episode (depressive or manic) is present for the majority of the illness, BUT there must be a period of **>=2 weeks of delusions or hallucinations WITHOUT a prominent mood episode**. That 2-week psychosis-without-mood window is what separates it from a mood disorder with psychotic features.

**Delusional disorder** involves **>=1 delusion for >=1 month** with NO other Criterion A symptoms (functioning is otherwise relatively preserved and behaviour is not obviously bizarre). Subtypes: **erotomanic** (another person, often of higher status, is in love with the patient), **grandiose**, **jealous** (the "Othello syndrome," delusion of a partner's infidelity), **persecutory** (most common), and **somatic**.

## 2. Bipolar and Related Disorders

These hinge on episode definitions. A **manic episode** is a distinct period of abnormally elevated, expansive, or irritable mood with increased energy, lasting **>=7 days (or any duration if hospitalisation is required)**, plus **>=3 of 7** symptoms (4 if mood is only irritable): inflated self-esteem/grandiosity, decreased need for sleep, pressured speech, flight of ideas, distractibility, increased goal-directed activity/psychomotor agitation, and excessive risky pleasurable activity (often abbreviated DIG FAST). A **hypomanic episode** is the same symptom set but milder, lasting **>=4 consecutive days**, observable but NOT causing marked impairment, hospitalisation, or psychosis.

| Disorder | Defining requirement | Duration gate |
|---|---|---|
| **Bipolar I** | At least one lifetime **manic** episode (MDE not required) | Manic episode **>=7 days** or hospitalisation |
| **Bipolar II** | At least one **hypomanic** episode AND at least one major depressive episode; never a full manic episode | Hypomania **>=4 days** |
| **Cyclothymic disorder** | Numerous periods of hypomanic and depressive symptoms not meeting full episode criteria | **>=2 years** in adults (**>=1 year** in children/adolescents), symptoms present at least half the time, no symptom-free gap >2 months |

**Rapid cycling** is a specifier (not a separate disorder): **>=4 mood episodes (manic, hypomanic, or depressive) within 12 months**. It is associated with poorer treatment response.

## 3. Depressive Disorders

### Major Depressive Disorder (MDD)

MDD requires **>=5 of 9 symptoms during the same 2-week period**, representing a change from previous functioning, and **at least one symptom must be either (1) depressed mood or (2) anhedonia** (loss of interest/pleasure). The nine (mnemonic SIG E CAPS): depressed mood, anhedonia, significant weight/appetite change, sleep disturbance (insomnia or hypersomnia), psychomotor agitation/retardation, fatigue/loss of energy, feelings of worthlessness or excessive guilt, diminished concentration/indecisiveness, and recurrent thoughts of death or suicidal ideation.

### Persistent Depressive Disorder (Dysthymia)

**Persistent depressive disorder (PDD)** is chronic, low-grade depression: depressed mood for **>=2 years (>=1 year in children/adolescents)**, present more days than not, with **>=2** of six symptoms (poor appetite or overeating, insomnia or hypersomnia, low energy, low self-esteem, poor concentration, hopelessness). During the 2-year span the person is never symptom-free for more than **2 months at a time**. PDD consolidated the older DSM-IV categories of dysthymia and chronic major depression.

### Disruptive Mood Dysregulation Disorder (DMDD)

**DMDD** was introduced in DSM-5 to curb over-diagnosis of paediatric bipolar disorder. Core features: severe recurrent temper outbursts (verbal or behavioural) grossly out of proportion to the situation, occurring **>=3 times per week** on average, with persistently irritable/angry mood between outbursts. Duration **>=12 months** (no symptom-free period >3 months), present in **>=2 of 3 settings**, **onset before age 10**, and the diagnosis is given only between ages **6 and 18**.

### Depressive specifiers

- **With melancholic features**: profound anhedonia/non-reactivity of mood, worse in the morning (diurnal variation), early-morning awakening, marked psychomotor changes, anorexia/weight loss, excessive guilt.
- **With atypical features**: **mood reactivity** (mood brightens to positive events) plus >=2 of: increased appetite/weight gain, hypersomnia, leaden paralysis, long-standing interpersonal rejection sensitivity. Note the reversal: atypical depression OVER-eats and OVER-sleeps.
- **With peripartum onset**: onset during pregnancy or within **4 weeks postpartum**.

## 4. Anxiety Disorders

Anxiety disorders share excessive fear (response to real or perceived imminent threat) and anxiety (anticipation of future threat).

- **Panic disorder**: recurrent **unexpected panic attacks** (abrupt surge of intense fear peaking within minutes, with >=4 of 13 symptoms such as palpitations, sweating, trembling, dyspnoea, chest pain, derealisation, fear of losing control or dying), followed by **>=1 month** of persistent worry about further attacks or maladaptive behaviour change.
- **Generalised Anxiety Disorder (GAD)**: excessive, hard-to-control worry about multiple domains, more days than not for **>=6 months**, with **>=3 of 6** somatic/cognitive symptoms (restlessness, easily fatigued, difficulty concentrating, irritability, muscle tension, sleep disturbance). In children only 1 symptom is required.
- **Specific phobia**: marked fear of a specific object/situation (animal, natural environment, blood-injection-injury, situational, other), almost always immediate, actively avoided, **>=6 months**, out of proportion to actual danger.
- **Social anxiety disorder (social phobia)**: fear of social/performance situations involving possible scrutiny and negative evaluation, **>=6 months**; may specify "performance only."
- **Agoraphobia**: marked fear/anxiety about **>=2 of 5 situations**: (1) using public transport, (2) being in open spaces, (3) being in enclosed places, (4) standing in line or being in a crowd, (5) being outside the home alone. The fear is that escape may be difficult or help unavailable if panic-like symptoms occur; duration **>=6 months**.
- **Separation anxiety disorder**: developmentally inappropriate fear of separation from attachment figures, **>=4 weeks in children/adolescents** and **>=6 months in adults**. No longer classified as childhood-only in DSM-5.

## 5. Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorders

**OCD** is defined by **obsessions** (recurrent, intrusive, unwanted thoughts/urges/images causing anxiety) and/or **compulsions** (repetitive behaviours or mental acts performed to neutralise the obsession or prevent a feared event, not realistically connected or clearly excessive). They are **time-consuming (>1 hour/day)** or cause significant distress/impairment. DSM-5 added **insight specifiers**: with **good or fair insight**, with **poor insight**, and with **absent insight / delusional beliefs**. A tic-related specifier also exists.

- **Body dysmorphic disorder (BDD)**: preoccupation with one or more perceived defects in physical appearance that are not observable or appear slight to others, plus repetitive behaviours (mirror checking, grooming, reassurance-seeking) or mental acts (comparing). A muscle dysmorphia specifier exists.
- **Hoarding disorder**: persistent difficulty discarding possessions regardless of value, due to a perceived need to save and distress at discarding, leading to accumulation that congests living areas. Specify "with excessive acquisition."
- **Trichotillomania (hair-pulling disorder)**: recurrent pulling out of one's hair leading to hair loss, with repeated attempts to stop.
- **Excoriation (skin-picking) disorder**: recurrent skin picking resulting in lesions, with repeated attempts to stop.

Trichotillomania and excoriation are **body-focused repetitive behaviours**; unlike OCD compulsions, they are not driven by obsessions.

## 6. Trauma- and Stressor-Related Disorders

These uniquely require an identifiable stressor as a diagnostic precondition.

### PTSD versus Acute Stress Disorder

Both follow exposure to actual or threatened death, serious injury, or sexual violence (directly, witnessed, learned of in a close other, or repeated exposure to aversive details). **PTSD** (in adults) requires symptoms from **four clusters**:
1. **Intrusion**: recurrent involuntary memories, nightmares, flashbacks, distress/physiological reactivity to cues (>=1 symptom).
2. **Avoidance**: of internal reminders (thoughts/feelings) or external reminders (people/places) (>=1 symptom).
3. **Negative alterations in cognitions and mood**: amnesia for the event, persistent negative beliefs, distorted blame, persistent negative emotions, diminished interest, detachment, inability to feel positive emotions (>=2 symptoms).
4. **Alterations in arousal and reactivity**: irritability/aggression, recklessness, hypervigilance, exaggerated startle, concentration problems, sleep disturbance (>=2 symptoms).

The disturbance lasts **>1 month**. Specifiers: **with dissociative symptoms** (depersonalisation/derealisation) and **with delayed expression** (full criteria not met until >=6 months post-event).

**Acute Stress Disorder (ASD)** has the same triggering criterion but is the short-duration analogue: **3 days to 1 month** after exposure, requiring **>=9 symptoms** drawn from five categories (intrusion, negative mood, dissociation, avoidance, arousal). The clean gate: **symptoms 3 days to 1 month = ASD; persisting >1 month = PTSD.**

### Reactive Attachment Disorder versus Disinhibited Social Engagement Disorder

Both arise from **pathogenic care** (social neglect, repeated changes of caregivers, institutional rearing) in early childhood, with developmental age **>=9 months**.
- **Reactive Attachment Disorder (RAD)**: the child is **inhibited and emotionally withdrawn** toward caregivers, rarely seeks or responds to comfort. It is an **internalising** pattern resembling depression.
- **Disinhibited Social Engagement Disorder (DSED)**: the child is **overly familiar with unfamiliar adults**, willing to wander off with a stranger, lacking normal reticence. It is an **externalising / disinhibited** pattern and can persist even after secure attachments form.

### Adjustment disorders

**Adjustment disorder** is the diagnosis when emotional or behavioural symptoms develop in response to an identifiable stressor (not necessarily traumatic) **within 3 months of onset** of the stressor, are out of proportion to the stressor or cause significant impairment, and do NOT meet criteria for another disorder. Once the stressor (or its consequences) ends, symptoms **resolve within 6 months**. Subtypes specify the predominant feature: with depressed mood, with anxiety, with mixed anxiety and depressed mood, with disturbance of conduct, with mixed disturbance of emotions and conduct, or unspecified.

## High-Yield Recap

- **Psychotic duration gating**: brief psychotic **<1 month**; schizophreniform **1 to 6 months**; schizophrenia **>=6 months total with >=1 month active phase** plus functional decline.
- Schizophrenia Criterion A: **>=2 of 5** symptoms, at least one from delusions/hallucinations/disorganised speech.
- Five A's of negative symptoms: **avolition, alogia, anhedonia, asociality, affective flattening**; DSM-5 core domains = diminished expression + avolition.
- Schneider FRS include thought insertion/withdrawal/broadcasting, passivity phenomena, third-person voices, delusional perception.
- Catatonia: **>=3 of 12** signs (waxy flexibility, echolalia, echopraxia, stupor, etc.); treat with benzodiazepines / ECT.
- Schizoaffective: mood episode for most of illness BUT **>=2 weeks of psychosis without prominent mood**.
- Delusional disorder: **>=1 delusion, >=1 month**, no other Criterion A symptoms; persecutory subtype most common.
- **Mania >=7 days** (or hospitalisation), **>=3/7** symptoms; **hypomania >=4 days**. Bipolar I = mania; Bipolar II = hypomania + MDE.
- Cyclothymia: **>=2 years adults / >=1 year youth**. Rapid cycling: **>=4 episodes / 12 months**.
- MDD: **>=5/9 symptoms over 2 weeks**, mood OR anhedonia mandatory (SIG E CAPS).
- PDD/dysthymia: **>=2 years (>=1 year youth)**, never symptom-free >2 months.
- DMDD: outbursts **>=3x/week**, **>=12 months**, **>=2 settings**, **onset before 10**, diagnosed ages 6 to 18.
- Specifiers: melancholic (worse AM, non-reactive); atypical (mood reactivity + overeating + hypersomnia); peripartum (within 4 weeks postpartum).
- GAD: worry **>=6 months + >=3/6** somatic symptoms. Panic disorder: unexpected attacks + **>=1 month** of worry/avoidance.
- Agoraphobia: fear of **>=2 of 5** situations. Separation anxiety adult duration **>=6 months**.
- OCD: obsessions and/or compulsions, **>1 hour/day**; insight specifiers (good/fair, poor, absent-delusional).
- BDD, hoarding, trichotillomania, excoriation are OCD-related; hair/skin disorders are body-focused repetitive behaviours, not obsession-driven.
- **PTSD = 4 clusters (intrusion, avoidance, negative cognition/mood, arousal), >1 month.** ASD = **3 days to 1 month, >=9 symptoms**.
- RAD = inhibited/withdrawn (internalising); DSED = indiscriminately friendly (disinhibited). Both need developmental age **>=9 months** and pathogenic care.
- Adjustment disorder: onset **within 3 months** of stressor, resolves **within 6 months** of stressor ending.
