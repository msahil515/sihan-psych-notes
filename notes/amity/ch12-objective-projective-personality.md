# Ch 12: Objective & Projective Personality Typologies

This chapter covers the two great families of personality assessment that dominate the exam: **objective tests** (structured, self-report, fixed-response instruments like the MMPI-2 and MCMI-IV) and **projective techniques** (ambiguous-stimulus methods like the Rorschach and the TAT). You will be tested on item counts, scale names and numbers, code types, scoring categories, and the theoretical assumptions behind each method, so this chapter teaches every component precisely.

## Objective vs Projective: The Core Distinction

An **objective personality test** uses standardized stimuli, a fixed response format (true/false, Likert, multiple choice), and an objective scoring key that does not depend on the examiner's judgment. Scores are referenced against normative data, so the method is high in reliability and replicability. The MMPI-2 and MCMI-IV are the archetypes.

A **projective technique** presents deliberately **ambiguous, unstructured stimuli** (inkblots, vague pictures, incomplete sentences). It rests on the **projective hypothesis**: when a stimulus is unstructured, the person imposes their own needs, conflicts, and unconscious organization onto it, so the response reveals personality structure that the person cannot or will not report directly. Scoring requires trained clinical judgment, which lowers reliability but is argued to increase access to defended, unconscious material.

| Feature | Objective (MMPI-2, MCMI-IV) | Projective (Rorschach, TAT) |
|---|---|---|
| Stimulus | Structured, clear | Ambiguous, unstructured |
| Response format | Fixed (T/F, ratings) | Free, open-ended |
| Scoring | Objective key, normed | Clinical judgment, systems |
| Theory base | Empirical / trait | Psychodynamic / perceptual |
| Reliability | High | Lower (improved by systems) |
| Main risk | Faking, response sets | Subjectivity, weak validity |

**Strengths of objective tests**: economical, reliable, normed, validity scales detect distortion. **Limitations**: vulnerable to response sets and deliberate faking, surface-level, require literacy and cooperation. **Strengths of projectives**: bypass conscious censoring, rich qualitative data, hard to fake convincingly. **Limitations**: lower reliability, validity debates, time-intensive, examiner-dependent.

## MMPI-2 (Minnesota Multiphasic Personality Inventory, 2nd ed.)

The **MMPI-2** is the most widely used objective personality inventory. It contains **567 true/false items** and is the 1989 restandardization of the original MMPI on a sample of 2,600 community adults. It was built by **empirical (criterion) keying**: items were retained not for face content but because they statistically separated clinical groups from normals. Authored originally by **Starke Hathaway and J. C. McKinley**. Required reading level is about sixth grade; administration takes 60 to 90 minutes.

### Validity Scales

Validity scales detect the test-taking attitude and response distortion that would invalidate the clinical profile.

- **L (Lie)**: 15 items tapping claims of near-perfect virtue. High L means naive, unsophisticated faking-good (the person denies common human faults).
- **F (Infrequency)**: items rarely endorsed by normals. High F suggests faking-bad, severe psychopathology, random responding, or a cry for help.
- **K (Correction/Defensiveness)**: a more subtle defensiveness scale than L; sophisticated minimization of problems. K is also added back (the **K-correction**) to several clinical scales to compensate for guardedness.
- **Fb (Back F)**: infrequency items placed in the **second half** of the booklet; flags a respondent who started attentively but deteriorated or stopped reading later.
- **VRIN (Variable Response Inconsistency)**: scores pairs of items with similar or opposite content; high VRIN signals random, inconsistent responding.
- **TRIN (True Response Inconsistency)**: pairs of opposite-content items; detects **acquiescence** (yea-saying, all-true) or **nay-saying** (all-false) response sets.
- **Fp (Infrequency-Psychopathology)**: infrequency items rare even among psychiatric inpatients; high Fp points to over-reporting/malingering rather than genuine severe illness.
- **S (Superlative Self-Presentation)**: measures the claiming of unusual virtue and adjustment; another faking-good/defensiveness indicator.

The classic **L-F-K configuration**: an inverted-V or "?/L high, F low, K high" pattern suggests defensiveness; the inverse (F high, L and K low) suggests exaggeration or distress.

### The 10 Clinical Scales

Each scale has a number and a code; clinicians refer to them by **number** to avoid reifying the old label. Scores are reported as **T-scores** (mean 50, SD 10); **T ≥ 65** is the clinically significant cutoff on the MMPI-2.

| # | Code | Name | What elevation suggests |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hs | Hypochondriasis | Excessive bodily concern, somatic complaints |
| 2 | D | Depression | Dysphoria, pessimism, low morale |
| 3 | Hy | Hysteria | Conversion symptoms, denial, need for affection |
| 4 | Pd | Psychopathic Deviate | Rebelliousness, conflict with authority, impulsivity |
| 5 | Mf | Masculinity-Femininity | Interest patterns vs gender norms (scored by sex) |
| 6 | Pa | Paranoia | Suspiciousness, sensitivity, ideas of reference |
| 7 | Pt | Psychasthenia | Anxiety, obsessions, doubt, guilt |
| 8 | Sc | Schizophrenia | Unusual thinking, alienation, perceptual oddities |
| 9 | Ma | Hypomania | Overactivity, elevated mood, grandiosity |
| 0 | Si | Social Introversion | Shyness, withdrawal vs sociability |

### Code Types (2-Point and 3-Point)

A **code type** names the two or three highest clinical scales (in descending order). Interpreting configurations is more valid than single-scale reading.

- **2-7 (D-Pt)**: the most common clinical code; **anxiety plus depression**, worry, tension, self-criticism, rumination. Highly distressed, motivated for treatment.
- **4-9 (Pd-Ma)**: the **acting-out / antisocial** profile; impulsivity, poor judgment, sensation seeking, low guilt, conflict with authority.
- **1-3 (Hs-Hy)** with **scale 2 lower** produces the **conversion V**: scales 1 and 3 are elevated while 2 dips between them, the hallmark of **conversion disorder** with somatic complaints maintained by denial (la belle indifférence).
- **2-7-8 (D-Pt-Sc)**: severe distress with thought disturbance, anxiety, depression, and possible psychotic features; chronic, treatment-resistant presentations.

## MCMI-IV (Millon Clinical Multiaxial Inventory, 4th ed.)

The **MCMI-IV** is an objective inventory built specifically to assess **personality disorders and clinical syndromes**, not general personality. It is grounded in **Theodore Millon's evolutionary theory of personality**. Millon argued that personality styles arise from how a person navigates four **evolutionary polarities**: (1) **existence** (pleasure vs pain, life-enhancement vs life-preservation), (2) **adaptation** (active vs passive modes of adjusting to the environment), (3) **replication** (self vs other, individuation vs nurturance), and later a (4) **abstraction/cognition** polarity. Each personality pattern reflects a particular imbalance or rigidity across these polarities (for example, the dependent style is passive and other-oriented; the antisocial style is active and self-oriented).

The MCMI-IV aligns with **DSM** personality-disorder constructs, has roughly **195 true/false items**, and is intended for adults already in clinical settings (it should not be used as a general screening tool on normals). It uses **Base Rate (BR) scores** rather than T-scores. BR scores anchor cutoffs to the actual prevalence of each disorder: **BR 75** indicates the presence of a trait/syndrome and **BR 85** indicates prominence, so the test is calibrated to real-world frequency instead of assuming a normal distribution. It includes scales for **Clinical Personality Patterns**, **Severe Personality Pathology**, **Clinical Syndromes**, **Severe Clinical Syndromes**, and **Modifying Indices** (validity).

## Rorschach Inkblot Test (Exner Comprehensive System)

The **Rorschach**, created by **Hermann Rorschach** (1921), presents **10 standardized inkblots** (5 achromatic black/grey, 2 black-and-red, 3 fully chromatic). **John Exner** integrated the five competing scoring schools into the **Comprehensive System (CS)**, giving the test empirical scoring and norms. Administration has two phases: the **Response (Free Association) phase** ("What might this be?") and the **Inquiry phase**, where the examiner asks "Where is it?" and "What makes it look like that?" to code each response.

Every response is coded on several dimensions:

### Location
Where on the blot the percept was seen: **W** = whole blot used; **D** = a common, frequently-used detail; **Dd** = an unusual/small detail; **S** = white space used (often linked to oppositionality or anger). **Developmental Quality (DQ)** rates how the location is organized: **+** (synthesized, two objects in meaningful relationship), **o** (ordinary single object), **v/+** and **v** (vague, no specific form demand), reflecting cognitive sophistication.

### Determinants
The features of the blot that *determined* the response (the heart of CS interpretation):
- **F (Form)**: pure shape, the most common determinant; high pure-F (high **Lambda**) suggests a constricted, simplistic coping style.
- **M (Human Movement)**: seeing humans in action; linked to ideation, fantasy, and inner resources. **FM** = animal movement (instinctual need states). **m** = inanimate movement (situational stress, tension).
- **Color**: **FC** (form dominant over color = modulated affect), **CF** (color dominant = less controlled emotion), **C** (pure color = raw, unmodulated affect). The FC:CF+C ratio indexes affect regulation.
- **C' (achromatic color)**: black/grey/white used as color; associated with constrained or internalized, painful affect (dysphoria).
- **Shading**: **T (texture)** = needs for closeness/dependency; **V (vista/depth)** = painful self-scrutiny; **Y (diffuse shading)** = anxiety, helplessness.
- **FD (Form Dimension)**: depth/perspective by form alone; introspection.
- **Pairs (2) and Reflections (rF/Fr)**: based on the symmetry of the blots; reflections are linked to **narcissism/egocentricity**, pairs to self-focus.

### Form Quality, Content, Populars
- **Form Quality (FQ)** rates how well the percept fits the blot's actual shape: **+** (superior), **o** (ordinary/conventional), **u** (unusual but plausible), **-** (minus, distorted, poor reality testing). A high proportion of FQ- (poor **X-%**) indicates impaired reality testing.
- **Content** categories: **H** (whole human), **(H)** (fictional/mythical human), **Hd** (human detail), **A** (animal), **Ad**, **An** (anatomy), **Bl** (blood), **Sex**, and so on.
- **Populars (P)**: the small set of responses given by most people (for example, the bat or butterfly on Card I, the two humans on Card III). Number of Populars indexes conventionality and conformity to common perception.

### The Structural Summary
The coded responses are tallied into a **Structural Summary** of ratios and indices:
- **Lambda (L)** = pure-F responses ÷ all other responses; high Lambda = avoidant, simplistic, narrow engagement.
- **Erlebnistypus (EB)** = the ratio of **Human Movement (M)** to **weighted Color (WSumC)**. **Introversive** style (M > color): the person uses inner life, ideation, and fantasy to solve problems. **Extratensive** style (color > M): the person relies on emotional, interpersonal interaction with the environment. An **ambitent** has no clear preference and tends to be less efficient.
- **EA vs es**: **EA (Experience Actual)** = the sum of organized resources (M + WSumC) available for coping; **es (Experienced Stimulation)** = the press of unmet needs and distress (FM + m + shading sums). EA > es means resources exceed demands.
- **D-score and Adjusted D (AdjD)**: derived from EA minus es; indexes **stress tolerance and control**. A negative D-score signals current overload of resources by stress; AdjD removes situational stress to show chronic capacity.

**Constellations (special composite indices)** flag clinical conditions:
- **CDI (Coping Deficit Index)**: social/coping immaturity and ineffectiveness.
- **DEPI (Depression Index)**: affective/depressive features.
- **SCZI**, replaced by the **PTI (Perceptual-Thinking Index)**: disordered thinking and impaired reality testing (psychosis spectrum).
- **HVI (Hypervigilance Index)**: guardedness, mistrust, paranoid readiness.
- **S-CON (Suicide Constellation)**: an 8-of-12 cutoff combination empirically associated with completed suicide; a positive S-CON is a serious clinical alert.

## Thematic Apperception Test (TAT)

The **TAT**, developed by **Henry Murray and Christiana Morgan** (1935) at the Harvard Psychological Clinic, presents ambiguous **black-and-white picture cards** (31 in the full set, of which about 20 are selected for a given examinee, some specific to age and sex) plus one blank card. The person is asked to tell a **story** for each picture: what is happening, what led up to it, what the characters are thinking and feeling, and how it turns out. The assumption is that the storyteller identifies with a character and projects their own dynamics into the narrative.

Interpretation uses **Murray's need-press model** from his **personology**:
- **Hero**: the character (usually the protagonist) with whom the storyteller identifies; the hero's experience is read as the examinee's own.
- **Needs (internal forces driving the hero)**: most assessed are **nAch (need for Achievement)** (striving to accomplish, master, excel, famously studied by McClelland), **nAff (need for Affiliation)** (desire for warm relationships and belonging), and **nAgg (need for Aggression)** (urge to harm, dominate, retaliate). Many other Murray needs exist (nurturance, dominance, autonomy, abasement).
- **Press (external/environmental forces acting on the hero)**: Murray distinguished **alpha press** (the environment as it objectively exists) from **beta press** (the environment as the hero subjectively perceives it). Beta press is more revealing of the person's perceptual world.
- **Thema**: the recurring interplay of a need and a press across stories (the need-press combination plus its outcome). A consistent **thema** across multiple cards reveals the person's enduring motivational patterns and conflicts.

Scoring is largely **qualitative/clinical** in standard use (formal systems like the **Defense Mechanisms Manual** and McClelland's nAch coding exist), which makes the TAT high in clinical richness but lower in standardized reliability.

## High-Yield Recap

- **Objective vs projective**: objective = structured stimulus, fixed response, objective key, high reliability; projective = ambiguous stimulus, free response, clinical scoring, relies on the **projective hypothesis**.
- **MMPI-2**: **567 true/false items**, empirically (criterion) keyed, Hathaway and McKinley; **T ≥ 65** is clinically significant.
- **MMPI-2 validity scales**: **L** (naive fake-good), **F** (fake-bad/distress), **K** (subtle defensiveness, K-correction), **Fb** (back-half infrequency), **VRIN** (random inconsistency), **TRIN** (yea/nay-saying), **Fp** (over-reporting beyond inpatients), **S** (superlative self-presentation).
- **10 clinical scales by number**: 1 Hs, 2 D, 3 Hy, 4 Pd, 5 Mf, 6 Pa, 7 Pt, 8 Sc, 9 Ma, 0 Si.
- **Code types**: **2-7** = anxiety + depression (most common); **4-9** = acting-out/antisocial; **1-3 with low 2** = **conversion V**; **2-7-8** = severe distress with thought disturbance.
- **MCMI-IV**: based on **Millon's evolutionary theory** (polarities: pleasure-pain, active-passive, self-other); assesses personality disorders; uses **Base Rate scores** (**BR 75** = present, **BR 85** = prominent).
- **Rorschach**: **10 inkblots** (Hermann Rorschach 1921); **Exner Comprehensive System**; Response + Inquiry phases.
- **Rorschach codes**: Location **W/D/Dd/S**; **DQ** (+, o, v); determinants **F, M/FM/m, FC/CF/C, C', T/V/Y, FD, pairs/reflections**; **Form Quality** +/o/u/-; **Populars**.
- **Structural Summary**: **Lambda** (pure F ratio), **EB** = M:WSumC → **introversive** (M dominant) vs **extratensive** (color dominant) vs ambitent; **EA vs es**; **D-score/AdjD** = stress tolerance; constellations **CDI, DEPI, PTI (old SCZI), HVI, S-CON** (suicide, 8/12 cutoff).
- **TAT**: Murray and Morgan (1935), 31 cards (~20 used) + 1 blank; tell a story; analyze **hero**, **needs** (nAch, nAff, nAgg), **press** (**alpha** = objective vs **beta** = perceived), and recurring **thema**.
