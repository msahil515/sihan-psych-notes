# Ch 13: Cognitive-Behavioral & Third-Wave Frameworks

This chapter traces the three "waves" of behavior therapy: first wave (classical behavioral techniques rooted in learning theory), second wave (cognitive therapies that target thought content), and third wave (acceptance and mindfulness-based therapies that target the relationship to thoughts rather than their content). Entrance exams lean heavily on matching figures to models, knowing exact components (the cognitive triad, the ABCDE model, the DBT modules), and distinguishing flooding from systematic desensitization, so memorize the named lists precisely.

## 1. First Wave: Behavioral Therapy

### Wolpe: Reciprocal Inhibition and Systematic Desensitization
**Joseph Wolpe** (1958) proposed the principle of **reciprocal inhibition**: a response (anxiety) can be weakened or eliminated if an antagonistic response (relaxation) is repeatedly elicited in the presence of the anxiety-provoking stimulus, because the two states are physiologically incompatible. You cannot be deeply relaxed and intensely anxious at the same moment. From this principle Wolpe built **systematic desensitization (SD)**, a graded counterconditioning procedure with three stages:
1. **Relaxation training**, typically **Jacobson's progressive muscle relaxation** (tense then release muscle groups).
2. Construction of an **anxiety hierarchy**: the client lists feared situations and ranks them from least to most frightening using **SUDS (Subjective Units of Distress Scale)**, a 0 to 100 scale where 0 is total calm and 100 is the worst imaginable panic.
3. **Graded pairing**: while deeply relaxed, the client imagines (or in vivo confronts) the lowest hierarchy item; once it triggers no anxiety, they move up one rung, ascending only after each lower item is mastered.

SD is the classic treatment for **specific phobias**. The exposure is **gradual and brief** and always paired with relaxation, which sharply distinguishes it from flooding.

### Skinner: Operant Conditioning and Its Clinical Applications
**B. F. Skinner's operant conditioning** holds that behavior is shaped by its consequences. The core diagnostic tool is **functional analysis** using the **ABC model**: **A**ntecedent (the trigger or setting event), **B**ehavior (the observable response), **C**onsequence (what follows and either reinforces or punishes the behavior). Identifying the ABC chain lets the therapist alter antecedents or consequences to change behavior.

Key reinforcement contingencies: **positive reinforcement** (adding a pleasant stimulus to increase a behavior), **negative reinforcement** (removing an aversive stimulus to increase a behavior), **positive punishment** (adding an aversive stimulus to decrease a behavior), and **negative punishment** (removing a pleasant stimulus, for example response cost or time-out, to decrease a behavior).

- **Token economy**: a structured program (used on wards, in classrooms, with intellectual disability) where desired behaviors earn **tokens** (secondary/conditioned reinforcers) that are later exchanged for **backup reinforcers** (real rewards). Foundational work by **Ayllon and Azrin** (1968).
- **Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA)**: the systematic application of operant principles, prominent in **autism** intervention. Two key teaching methods are **shaping** (reinforcing successive approximations toward a target behavior) and **chaining** (linking simple steps into a complex sequence, taught either forward or backward).
- **Premack principle**: a high-frequency (preferred) behavior can reinforce a low-frequency (less-preferred) behavior. "First eat your vegetables (low probability), then you may play (high probability)." Also called **Grandma's rule**.

### Exposure Therapies
Exposure works through **habituation** (the natural decline of a response with repeated unreinforced presentation) and **extinction** (breaking the learned stimulus-fear association).

| Technique | Pacing | Modality | Relaxation used? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Systematic desensitization** | Gradual, hierarchy | Imaginal or in vivo | Yes (relaxation paired) |
| **Flooding** | Maximal intensity, immediate | Usually in vivo | No |
| **Implosion (implosive therapy)** | Maximal intensity, immediate | Imaginal only, with psychodynamic/exaggerated themes | No |

- **Flooding** exposes the client directly to the most feared stimulus at full intensity and keeps them in it (without escape) until anxiety extinguishes on its own. **Implosive therapy** (Stampfl) is similar but purely **imaginal** and deliberately amplifies the feared imagery, often with hypothesized unconscious/psychodynamic content.
- **Exposure and Response Prevention (ERP)** is the gold-standard behavioral treatment for **OCD**. The client is exposed to the obsession-triggering cue (for example touching a "contaminated" doorknob) and then **prevented from performing the compulsive ritual** (washing). The ritual is what normally provides relief; blocking it allows anxiety to habituate and teaches that the feared catastrophe does not occur.

### Behavioral Activation (BA)
**Behavioral activation** is an evidence-based treatment for **depression**, derived from **Lewinsohn's** theory that depression is maintained by a loss of **response-contingent positive reinforcement** and by avoidance. The depressed person withdraws, which removes sources of reward and deepens low mood in a vicious cycle. BA uses **activity monitoring** and **activity scheduling** to deliberately re-engage the client in rewarding and mastery-giving activities, breaking the withdrawal-depression loop. It is notable for being effective without directly challenging thoughts.

### Biofeedback
**Biofeedback** gives clients real-time visual or auditory information about normally involuntary physiological processes (heart rate, muscle tension via **EMG**, skin conductance/GSR, brain waves via **EEG/neurofeedback**, skin temperature) so they can learn to consciously regulate them. It is used for tension headaches, migraine, hypertension, anxiety, and chronic pain. The principle is operant: the feedback signal acts as reinforcement for movement toward the target physiological state.

## 2. Second Wave: Cognitive Therapy

### Aaron Beck: The Cognitive Model
**Aaron T. Beck** developed **Cognitive Therapy (CT)** in the 1960s, originally for depression. Its central thesis is that **dysfunctional thinking is common to all psychological disturbance** and that emotion and behavior follow from how a person interprets events, not the events themselves.

**The Cognitive Triad** (Beck's model of depression) is a triad of negative views:
1. Negative view of the **self** ("I am worthless").
2. Negative view of the **world / current experience** ("Everything is against me").
3. Negative view of the **future** ("Things will never improve", hopelessness).

**Levels of cognition** (from deepest to most surface):
- **Schemas / core beliefs**: deep, absolute beliefs about self, others, and the world (for example "I am unlovable"), often formed early in life.
- **Intermediate beliefs**: the rules, attitudes, and assumptions that flow from core beliefs (for example "If I am not perfect, I will be rejected").
- **Automatic thoughts**: the spontaneous, situation-specific surface thoughts that pop up and directly drive emotion ("She didn't reply, she hates me").

**Cognitive distortions** (systematic errors in thinking) that exams ask about by exact name:

| Distortion | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Arbitrary inference** | Drawing a conclusion with no evidence or against the evidence. |
| **Selective abstraction** | Focusing on one negative detail while ignoring the larger context. |
| **Overgeneralization** | Drawing a sweeping rule from a single event ("I failed once, I always fail"). |
| **Dichotomous (all-or-nothing) thinking** | Seeing things in black-and-white extremes, no middle ground. |
| **Personalization** | Attributing external events to oneself without basis ("It's my fault it rained"). |
| **Magnification / Minimization** | Exaggerating negatives or shrinking positives; **catastrophizing** is extreme magnification. |

**Core techniques of Beckian CT**:
- **Collaborative empiricism**: therapist and client work as a team, treating beliefs as **hypotheses to be tested** against evidence rather than facts.
- **Socratic questioning (guided discovery)**: the therapist asks open questions ("What is the evidence for that thought? Is there another way to see it?") so the client reaches new insight themselves rather than being told.
- **Downward arrow technique**: repeatedly asking "If that were true, what would it mean about you?" to drill from a surface automatic thought down to the underlying core belief.

### Albert Ellis: Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy (REBT)
**Albert Ellis** founded **REBT** in 1955, predating Beck and considered the first formal cognitive therapy. Its centerpiece is the **ABCDE model**:
- **A** = **Activating event** (the trigger).
- **B** = **Belief** about the event (rational or irrational).
- **C** = **Consequence** (the emotional and behavioral result). Crucially, Ellis argued that **B causes C, not A**. It is the belief, not the event, that produces the disturbance.
- **D** = **Disputation**: actively challenging and arguing against the irrational belief.
- **E** = **Effective new philosophy / new emotional effect** that results from successful disputation.

Ellis held that disturbance comes from **irrational beliefs** built on rigid demands. He coined **"musturbation"** (the tyranny of "I must", "I should", "I have to") and emphasized rigid **shoulds and oughts**. A key downstream irrationality is **awfulizing / catastrophizing** and **low frustration tolerance ("I can't stand it")**. Disputation can be logical, empirical, or pragmatic. REBT is more **directive, confrontational, and philosophical** than Beck's gentler, more empirical Socratic style; this contrast is a favorite exam question.

## 3. Third Wave: Acceptance and Mindfulness-Based Therapies

The third wave shares an emphasis on **acceptance**, **mindfulness**, **values**, and changing one's **relationship** to inner experiences rather than changing their content.

### Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT)
**Marsha Linehan** developed **DBT** specifically for **Borderline Personality Disorder (BPD)** and chronic suicidality/self-harm. "Dialectical" refers to the central balance between **acceptance** (validating the client as they are) and **change** (pushing toward new behavior), and the synthesis of opposites. It draws on **Zen mindfulness** plus standard CBT. Full DBT has four treatment modes: individual therapy, group **skills training**, phone coaching, and a therapist consultation team. The **four skills-training modules** are the most tested:

| Module | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Core mindfulness** | Nonjudgmental awareness of the present; "wise mind" (balance of emotion mind and reasonable mind). The foundational module. |
| **Distress tolerance** | Surviving crises without making them worse (acceptance-oriented). |
| **Emotion regulation** | Understanding, reducing vulnerability to, and changing painful emotions (change-oriented). |
| **Interpersonal effectiveness** | Asserting needs and maintaining relationships and self-respect. |

Two named DBT skill acronyms:
- **DEAR MAN** (interpersonal effectiveness, for getting what you want): **D**escribe, **E**xpress, **A**ssert, **R**einforce, (stay) **M**indful, **A**ppear confident, **N**egotiate.
- **TIPP** (distress tolerance, for rapidly lowering extreme arousal): **T**emperature (cold water on the face triggers the dive reflex), **I**ntense exercise, **P**aced breathing, **P**aired (progressive) muscle relaxation.

### Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT)
**Steven C. Hayes** developed **ACT** (pronounced as one word, "act"), grounded in **Relational Frame Theory (RFT)**, a behavioral account of language and cognition holding that humans relate stimuli in arbitrary ways, which is why thoughts gain so much power to cause suffering. The goal of ACT is **psychological flexibility**: the ability to stay in contact with the present moment and act according to one's **values** even in the presence of unwanted thoughts and feelings. The model is the **Hexaflex**, six interlinked processes:

1. **Acceptance**: actively allowing painful internal experiences rather than fighting them (opposite of experiential avoidance).
2. **Cognitive defusion**: stepping back from thoughts, seeing them as just words/events ("I am having the thought that I am a failure") rather than literal truth.
3. **Contact with the present moment**: flexible, mindful attention to the here and now.
4. **Self-as-context**: the observing self, a stable vantage point from which one notices experiences without being defined by them (distinct from self-as-content, the conceptualized self).
5. **Values**: chosen life directions that give action meaning.
6. **Committed action**: taking concrete, values-guided steps even when it is uncomfortable.

A core ACT slogan is to **accept** internal experience, **choose** valued directions, and **take action**. Suffering is seen as worsened by **experiential avoidance** and **cognitive fusion**.

### Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy (MBCT)
**MBCT** was developed by **Segal, Williams, and Teasdale** by integrating **Beck's cognitive therapy** with **Jon Kabat-Zinn's Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR)**. Its primary, evidence-based purpose is the **prevention of relapse in recurrent depression**, especially for people who have had three or more previous episodes. It is delivered as an **8-week group program**. The mechanism: rather than disputing the content of depressive thoughts, clients learn to **observe thoughts as passing mental events** and to "decenter" from them, interrupting the automatic **ruminative cycle** that reactivates depression when low mood returns. It teaches recognizing early warning signs and shifting from the "doing mode" to the "being mode" of mind. **Kabat-Zinn's MBSR** itself (1979) was the parent program, originally for chronic pain and stress, also typically an 8-week course.

## High-Yield Recap
- **Wolpe**: reciprocal inhibition (relaxation inhibits anxiety) → **systematic desensitization** (relaxation + **anxiety hierarchy** ranked by **SUDS 0 to 100**, gradual). Treatment of choice for **specific phobia**.
- **Functional analysis = ABC**: Antecedent, Behavior, Consequence. **Token economy** (Ayllon and Azrin), **ABA** (shaping = successive approximations; chaining = linking steps). **Premack principle** = high-probability behavior reinforces low-probability behavior (Grandma's rule).
- **Flooding** = full-intensity, in vivo, no relaxation. **Implosion** = full-intensity, imaginal only, exaggerated themes. **ERP** = gold standard for **OCD** (expose to cue, prevent the ritual).
- **Behavioral activation** = for depression; restores response-contingent positive reinforcement (Lewinsohn). **Biofeedback** = real-time physiological feedback (EMG, GSR, EEG) for self-regulation.
- **Beck cognitive triad** = negative view of **self, world, future**. Cognition levels: **schemas/core beliefs → intermediate beliefs → automatic thoughts**. Techniques: **collaborative empiricism, Socratic questioning, downward arrow**.
- **Beck distortions**: arbitrary inference, selective abstraction, overgeneralization, dichotomous (all-or-nothing) thinking, personalization, magnification/minimization.
- **Ellis REBT**: **ABCDE** (Activating event, Belief, Consequence, Disputation, Effective new philosophy); **B causes C, not A**. Coined **"musturbation"** and the tyranny of **shoulds**. More directive/confrontational than Beck.
- **DBT** (Linehan, for **BPD**): dialectic of acceptance vs change; 4 modules = **core mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion regulation, interpersonal effectiveness**. **DEAR MAN** (interpersonal); **TIPP** = Temperature, Intense exercise, Paced breathing, Paired muscle relaxation (distress tolerance).
- **ACT** (Hayes, based on **Relational Frame Theory**): goal = **psychological flexibility**. **Hexaflex** = acceptance, defusion, present moment, self-as-context, values, committed action. Enemy = experiential avoidance and cognitive fusion.
- **MBCT** (Segal, Williams, Teasdale = Beck's CT + **Kabat-Zinn's MBSR**): **8-week** program for **relapse prevention in recurrent depression** (3+ episodes); decentering from ruminative thoughts.
